1. Fundamentals of GD&T

Meaning

Geometric Dimensioning and Tolerancing (GD&T) is a symbolic engineering language used to specify permissible geometric variation. It communicates functional requirements for the shape, orientation and location of features in addition to dimensional requirements.

ENGINEERING DRAWING Size dimensions ──► How large? GD&T ───────────► How accurately shaped / oriented / located? │ ├─ Form ├─ Orientation └─ Location / related geometry

Need

  • Clear communication of functional requirements.
  • Improved interchangeability and assembly.
  • Reduced ambiguity in drawings.
  • Better inspection planning and quality control.
  • Controlled geometric variation without unnecessarily tight size tolerances.

3. Datums

A datum is a theoretically exact reference such as a point, axis, line or plane used to establish a reference for measurement or geometric control. A datum feature is the physical feature selected to establish that reference.

PART ┌──────────────────────────┐ │ datum surface │ └──────────────────────────┘ ════════════════════════════ Datum A reference plane ↓ inspection reference

A datum reference frame provides a repeatable basis for locating and evaluating a component.

4. Straightness, Flatness and Roundness

StraightnessControls how straight a line element or derived axis is.
FlatnessControls whether a surface lies within two parallel planes separated by the tolerance.
RoundnessControls how closely a circular cross-section approaches a true circle.

Straightness — inspection

  1. Clean the component and setup.
  2. Establish the required reference condition.
  3. Traverse the measuring system along the specified line.
  4. Record readings at suitable positions.
  5. Identify maximum and minimum deviations.
  6. Determine the observed variation and compare it with the specified tolerance.
Measured surface: ~~~~~~~~/\\~~~~~~~ Reference line: ─────────────────── ↑ deviation

Typical observed variation from readings is maximum deviation minus minimum deviation, but the exact acceptance criterion must follow the specified drawing/assessment method.

Flatness — inspection

  1. Clean the surface.
  2. Place the component on a suitable reference setup.
  3. Use an appropriate surface-plate/indicator, precision-level or equivalent inspection method.
  4. Measure points over the required area or grid.
  5. Evaluate the maximum separation according to the selected method.
  6. Compare with the specified flatness tolerance.
Upper plane ─────────────────── Measured ~~~\____/~~~~\____~ Lower plane ─────────────────── ← flatness tolerance →

Roundness — inspection

  1. Select the required cross-sectional plane.
  2. Align and centre the component.
  3. Rotate the component/probe through the measurement range.
  4. Record radial displacement.
  5. Evaluate the profile using the specified roundness assessment method.
  6. Compare with the specified tolerance.

5. Worked Inspection Problem

Straightness variation

Given: deviations = +0.02, −0.01, +0.04, +0.01 and −0.03 mm.

Maximum = +0.04 mm

Minimum = −0.03 mm

Total variation = 0.04 − (−0.03) = 0.07 mm

Final: observed variation = 0.07 mm. Compare this with the specified tolerance and assessment method before deciding acceptance.

6. Surface Finish and Functionality

Surface texture consists of roughness, waviness, lay and flaws. It results from machining, forming, casting, grinding, vibration, tool wear and related processes.

  • Sliding surfaces: affects friction, wear and lubrication.
  • Sealing surfaces: affects contact and leakage.
  • Fatigue-sensitive surfaces: irregularities can influence stress concentration and fatigue.
  • Mating surfaces: affects real contact area, running-in and fit.

Important: smoother is not automatically better; the required texture depends on function and operating conditions.

8. Comparative Surface-Finish Measurement

The unknown surface is compared with suitable reference specimens of known or specified texture.

  1. Select appropriate reference specimens.
  2. Clean reference and test surfaces.
  3. Compare under controlled viewing/tactile conditions.
  4. Identify the closest matching reference or range.
  5. Record the result.

Advantages: quick, simple and inexpensive. Limitations: subjective and operator-dependent; it does not provide the same quantitative profile data as an instrumented method.

9. Stylus-Based Surface Finish Measurement

Principle

A fine stylus traverses the surface. Its vertical movement is converted into an electrical signal, amplified, filtered and processed to obtain surface parameters.

Surface → Stylus → Pickup/Transducer → Amplifier → Filter → Parameter calculation → Display

Full working

  1. Clean and position the workpiece.
  2. Set the appropriate traverse direction and measurement conditions.
  3. Bring the stylus into controlled contact.
  4. Traverse the specified length at controlled speed.
  5. Stylus displacement follows surface-profile variation.
  6. Pickup converts displacement to an electrical signal.
  7. Signal is amplified and filtered.
  8. Instrument calculates the selected parameter such as Ra.
  9. Result and measurement conditions are recorded.

Advantages: quantitative, repeatable and useful for process control. Limitations: contact can affect delicate surfaces; stylus geometry, direction and filtering influence results.

10. Optical Measurement Techniques

Optical methods obtain surface information using light rather than direct mechanical contact. Depending on the technique, interference, imaging, optical profiling or scattering may be used.

Light source → Surface → Detector / camera → Signal processing → Surface information

Advantages: non-contact, suitable for delicate surfaces and capable of dense spatial information. Limitations: reflectivity, transparency, contamination, optical alignment and lighting can affect results.

11. Filters

A measured profile contains components with different wavelengths. Filtering separates selected components so that roughness and longer-scale waviness/form components are evaluated appropriately.

Measured profile → Filtering / profile separation ├→ shorter-scale component → roughness └→ longer-scale component → waviness / form context
  • Filter selection affects reported roughness.
  • Cut-off and evaluation length must be considered together.
  • Filtering is part of defining the profile being evaluated, not simply noise removal.

12. 3D Surface Metrology

3D surface metrology extends a single 2D profile to an area-based topography map containing height information over two lateral coordinates.

Sa is an introductory areal parameter representing the arithmetic mean of absolute height deviations from the mean plane over the assessed area.

Sa = 1A ∬A |z(x,y)| dA

Do not confuse Sa (areal) with Ra (profile).

13. Full 12-Mark Answer — GD&T, Datums and Form Deviations

Introduction: Manufacturing produces dimensional and geometric variation. Dimensional tolerancing controls size while GD&T communicates allowable geometric variation and functional relationships.

1. GD&T: A standardized symbolic language for specifying geometric requirements.

2. Conventional vs GD&T: Conventional tolerancing primarily communicates dimensional limits; GD&T adds explicit geometric controls and datum references.

3. Datums: Theoretical references established from physical datum features to provide a repeatable inspection frame.

4. Straightness: Traverse a suitable measuring system along the required line, record deviations, find the observed variation and compare with tolerance.

5. Flatness: Measure the surface using a suitable method and determine whether all required points remain within the specified two-plane tolerance zone.

6. Roundness: Measure a selected circular cross-section, record radial variation and evaluate it using the specified criterion.

7. Precautions: clean the part, control temperature, align correctly, minimize vibration, use calibrated equipment and follow specified measurement conditions.

Conclusion: GD&T and geometric inspection control the geometry that directly affects fit and function.

14. Full 12-Mark Answer — Surface Finish Measurement

Introduction: Surface texture includes roughness, waviness, lay and flaws and directly influences friction, wear, lubrication, sealing, fatigue and contact.

1. Functionality: Select surface requirements according to the function of sliding, sealing, mating or fatigue-sensitive surfaces.

2. Parameters: Explain Ra, Rq, Rz, waviness and lay with the applicable evaluation convention.

3. Comparative method: Compare the test surface with suitable reference specimens; simple but subjective.

4. Stylus method: Stylus displacement → transducer → amplification → filtering → parameter calculation. It gives quantitative results.

5. Optical method: Uses light for non-contact surface information.

6. Filtering: Separates profile wavelength components for proper evaluation.

Conclusion: A surface-finish result should be interpreted together with its measurement method and evaluation conditions.

15. Full 12-Mark Answer — Stylus Instrument

Construction: stylus/pickup, traverse mechanism, signal-conditioning and amplification section, filtering/evaluation section and display/data-processing system.

Surface → Stylus → Pickup → Amplifier → Filter → Calculation → Display

Working:

  1. Clean and position workpiece.
  2. Align for correct traverse direction.
  3. Apply controlled stylus contact.
  4. Traverse specified length.
  5. Stylus follows profile.
  6. Pickup converts displacement to electrical signal.
  7. Amplify and filter signal.
  8. Calculate selected roughness parameter.
  9. Display and record result with conditions.

Precautions: suitable stylus force, clean surface, correct direction, correct cut-off/evaluation conditions, low vibration and calibrated instrument.

Conclusion: Stylus measurement provides quantitative surface-finish information when measurement conditions are correctly controlled.

16. Full 12-Mark Answer — 3D Surface Metrology

Step 1: A 2D profile measures one line and may miss spatial features outside that line.

Step 2: 3D metrology measures an area and represents height as a function of two lateral coordinates.

Step 3: A suitable instrument acquires the surface area and produces a topography map.

Step 4: Levelling, form removal, filtering and parameter calculation are performed according to the measurement procedure.

Step 5: Sa describes the arithmetic mean of absolute height deviations over the assessed area.

Step 6: Applications include surface comparison, process monitoring, quality control and functional surface characterization.

Conclusion: 3D metrology provides area-based information that can reveal surface structure missed by a single profile.

19. Common Exam Mistakes

  • Do not define GD&T as only dimensioning.
  • Do not confuse datum feature and theoretical datum.
  • Do not interchange straightness, flatness and roundness.
  • Do not equate surface finish with roughness alone.
  • Do not confuse profile Ra with areal Sa.
  • Do not omit traverse direction, filtering or evaluation conditions in a stylus answer.
  • For numerical answers write given → formula/criterion → substitution → calculation → units → final acceptance.
  • For 12-mark answers include a neat labelled schematic.
