1. Linear Measuring Instruments

1.1 Vernier Caliper

Diagram to reproduce in the exam: main scale, vernier scale, outside jaws, inside jaws, slider, locking screw and depth rod.

A vernier caliper is a direct-reading instrument used for external dimensions, internal dimensions, depth and step measurements. It combines a main scale with a sliding vernier scale so that fractions of a main-scale division can be read.

Construction

  • Main scale mounted on the beam.
  • Sliding jaw carrying the vernier scale.
  • Outside jaws for external dimensions.
  • Inside jaws for internal dimensions.
  • Depth rod for depth measurement.
  • Locking screw for retaining a setting.

Reading principle

The reading is obtained by combining the main-scale reading with the coincident vernier division. The exact least count depends on the particular vernier design.

Least Count = 1 Main-Scale Division − 1 Vernier-Scale Division

Precautions

  • Clean the jaws and workpiece.
  • Keep the jaws aligned with the measured feature.
  • Do not apply excessive force.
  • Avoid parallax while reading the scales.
  • Check zero before measurement.

1.2 Micrometer

A micrometer converts rotary motion of a precision screw into controlled axial movement of the spindle. It is widely used for accurate external dimensional measurement.

Main parts

Frame, anvil, spindle, sleeve/main scale, thimble, lock and ratchet stop.

Working

  1. Place the workpiece between anvil and spindle.
  2. Rotate the thimble to move the spindle.
  3. Use the ratchet to approach the work with approximately controlled measuring force.
  4. Read the sleeve and thimble scales according to the instrument graduation.
Exam distinction: least count/resolution describes the smallest scale increment; accuracy describes closeness to the reference value. A small least count does not automatically guarantee high accuracy.

1.3 Vernier Height Gauge

A height gauge is used on a surface plate to measure or mark vertical dimensions. A sliding carriage carries the vernier mechanism and a scriber or attachment.

1.4 Depth Micrometer

A depth micrometer measures depths of holes, slots, recesses and steps. The base is seated on the reference surface while the spindle extends into the feature.

1.5 Bore Gauge

A bore gauge is commonly used as a comparative instrument. It is set to a known reference dimension and the indicator shows the deviation of the bore from that setting.

1.6 Telescoping Gauge

A telescoping gauge is a transfer instrument for internal dimensions. The contact ends are expanded inside the bore, locked, removed and then measured with an external micrometer.

2. Gauge Blocks / Slip Gauges

Gauge blocks are precision length standards used for setting and checking measuring instruments, comparators and angular arrangements such as sine bars.

Wringing

Wringing is the controlled process of bringing two clean gauge-block faces into intimate contact by a slight sliding motion. Cleanliness, surface condition and careful handling are essential.

Selection of a gauge-block combination

  1. Start with the least significant required digit.
  2. Select a suitable block that removes that digit.
  3. Continue until the required dimension is obtained.
  4. Use as few blocks as practicable.

Precautions

  • Clean blocks before use.
  • Protect faces from scratches and corrosion.
  • Avoid unnecessary handling.
  • Separate wrung blocks by sliding rather than twisting.
  • Clean and protect blocks after use.

3. Comparators

A comparator compares a workpiece with a preset reference and indicates the deviation from that reference. It is mainly a comparison instrument rather than a direct absolute measuring instrument.

Mechanical comparator
Uses mechanical magnification through levers, gears or related mechanisms.
Pros: simple, robust, no external electrical supply.
Limits: friction, backlash, wear and limited range.
Electrical/electronic comparator
A displacement transducer produces an electrical signal that is amplified and displayed.
Pros: high magnification, small moving mass.
Limits: power supply and electrical/environmental sensitivity.
Pneumatic comparator
Dimensional changes alter pressure or flow at a nozzle and produce comparative indication.
Pros: useful for small variations and delicate surfaces.

Comparator characteristics

CharacteristicMeaning
MagnificationRatio of indicated movement to actual input displacement.
RangeUseful input-displacement span.
SensitivityChange in output for a change in input.
RepeatabilityConsistency under repeated measurement conditions.

4. Opto-Mechanical Measurements

4.1 Measuring Microscope

A measuring microscope provides optical magnification while calibrated stage or crosshair movement is used to determine small dimensions, angles and profiles.

4.2 Profile Projector

A profile projector optically enlarges the silhouette of a workpiece onto a screen. It is useful for small, delicate or complex profiles where direct contact is undesirable.

  • Profile comparison
  • Angle and radius checking
  • Small slots and formed features
  • Thread/profile inspection

5. Angular Measuring Instruments

5.1 Vernier Bevel Protractor

A bevel protractor is a direct-reading angular instrument consisting of a graduated circular scale, vernier arrangement, stock and movable blade. The blade is set against the feature and the angle is read from the graduated system.

5.2 Clinometer

A clinometer measures inclination or slope relative to a reference direction. It is useful for small angular deviations and machine-tool alignment work.

5.3 Angle Gauges

Angle gauges are precision angular standards. Blocks can be combined by addition and subtraction to obtain a required angle.

5.4 Precision Level

A precision level detects very small angular deviations of machine-tool beds, tables and reference surfaces.

5.5 Sine Bar

A sine bar uses a right-triangle relationship. The distance between roller centres is fixed. One roller is raised by a known slip-gauge height.

sin θ = hL    and    h = L sin θ

where h is the slip-gauge height and L is the distance between roller centres.

Full-working example

Given: L = 200 mm, required angle θ = 30°.

Formula: h = L sin θ.

Substitution: h = 200 × sin 30°.

Calculation: h = 200 × 0.5 = 100 mm.

Answer: A slip-gauge stack height of 100 mm is required under the raised roller, neglecting practical corrections.

Sine-bar errors and precautions

  • Roller-centre distance error
  • Slip-gauge dimensional error
  • Surface flatness and parallelism
  • Workpiece contact/deformation
  • Incorrect alignment

For practical measurement, use suitable angle ranges and maintain clean, stable contact conditions.

5.6 Autocollimator

An autocollimator is an optical instrument for measuring very small angular deviations. A collimated beam is directed toward a reflector and the reflected image is observed through the optical system.

For a small reflector tilt θ, the reflected beam direction changes by approximately 2θ. This optical doubling makes the instrument sensitive to small angular changes.

Applications: straightness, flatness, squareness, alignment, angular indexing and small angular comparisons.

5.7 Angle Dekkor

An angle dekkor is an optical angular-comparison instrument used to measure small angular deviations from a reference setting.

5.8 Alignment Telescope

An alignment telescope establishes a reference line for checking the alignment of machine-tool elements and other components over a distance.

6. Measurement of Screw Threads

Screw-thread measurement checks the geometry needed for correct fit and function. The important quantities include major diameter, minor diameter, effective/pitch diameter, pitch, lead, flank angle and thread form.

6.1 Thread terminology

TermMeaning
Major diameterLargest diameter of an external thread, measured over the crests.
Minor diameterDiameter associated with the thread roots.
PitchAxial distance between corresponding points on adjacent threads.
LeadAxial advance in one revolution. For a single-start thread, lead equals pitch.
Effective diameterDiameter associated with the functional flank geometry and thread fit.
Thread angleAngle defining the thread flank form.

6.2 Three-wire method

The three-wire method is a precision comparative method for determining effective diameter of an external screw thread. Two equal wires are placed on one side and one wire on the opposite side. A micrometer measures the dimension over the wires.

  1. Select suitable precision wires.
  2. Place two wires on one flank side and one on the opposite side.
  3. Position them so that they contact the thread flanks correctly.
  4. Measure over the wires using a micrometer.
  5. Use the appropriate thread geometry relation to determine effective diameter.

The best-wire condition aims to position the contact near the pitch line for the selected thread form.

7. Gear Measurement

Gear measurement verifies dimensional and functional accuracy before a gear is accepted for service.

7.1 Analytical measurement

Individual gear elements are measured separately to identify the source and magnitude of errors.

  • Runout
  • Pitch variation
  • Tooth profile
  • Tooth thickness
  • Lead

7.2 Functional checking

Functional checking evaluates the actual rolling/meshing behaviour of the gear pair. It reveals the combined effect of individual errors during operation.

AnalyticalFunctional
Measures individual gear elements.Checks actual rolling/meshing behaviour.
Helps identify the source of dimensional error.Shows the combined functional effect.
