ME3591 — Unit III: Design of Temporary and Permanent Joints

ACADRIX • Anna University R-2021 • Semester 5

Official syllabus scope: Threaded fasteners; bolted and eccentrically loaded bolted joints; knuckle and cotter joints; welded joints including butt, fillet and parallel transverse fillet welds under bending, torsion and eccentric loading; riveted structural joints; theory of bonded joints.

Official syllabus source: Anna University R-2021 B.E. Mechanical Engineering PDF. Use the permitted PSG Design Data Book for standard dimensions and tabulated design data where required.

1. Threaded Fasteners

Threaded fasteners are detachable joints used to connect machine parts. Important design ideas are preload, service load, bolt tension, clamped-member stiffness and the possible failure of the bolt or threads.

Exam points

  • Purpose: detachable connection and easy maintenance.
  • Preload helps maintain contact between connected members.
  • Service loading changes the bolt and joint-member forces according to their relative stiffness.
  • Consider tensile, shear and thread-related failure where applicable.

2. Bolted and Eccentrically Loaded Bolted Joints

For concentric loading, the applied load acts through the bolt-group centroid. For eccentric loading, the joint carries both the direct load and the moment produced by the eccentricity.

M = P × e

Exam procedure

  1. Draw the bolt arrangement and locate its centroid.
  2. Determine the direct load shared by the bolts.
  3. Calculate the eccentric moment.
  4. Determine the additional load caused by the moment.
  5. Combine direct and moment-induced effects at the critical bolt.
  6. Check the governing bolt/joint condition and adopt practical dimensions.

3. Knuckle Joint

A knuckle joint connects two rods carrying tensile load while permitting limited angular movement. The principal components are the rod, single eye, fork and knuckle pin.

Failure checks

  • Rod — tensile failure.
  • Pin — shear and bending as applicable.
  • Single eye — tearing and bearing.
  • Fork — tearing, shear and bearing.
  • Joint dimensions — final check against all governing conditions.

Exam tip: Always draw the complete joint and identify each section before writing equations.

4. Cotter Joint

A cotter joint connects two coaxial rods carrying axial load. A tapered cotter provides a detachable connection and resists separation.

Important components

  • Rod/spigot
  • Socket
  • Cotter

Design checks

Check the rod, spigot, socket and cotter against the relevant tensile, shear, bearing and crushing conditions specified by the design procedure. Calculate theoretical dimensions first, then adopt practical/standard dimensions and re-check.

5. Welded Joints

Welded joints are permanent joints. Unit III specifically includes butt welds, fillet welds and parallel transverse fillet welds, including loading by bending, torsion and eccentric forces.

Fillet-weld throat

Effective throat thickness = 0.707 × weld size

General weld-group workflow

  1. Draw the weld group and locate its centroid.
  2. Calculate direct load effects.
  3. Calculate the moment from eccentricity where present.
  4. Determine the stress distribution due to bending or torsion.
  5. Combine the relevant direct and moment-induced stresses.
  6. Find the critical weld location.
  7. Compare with allowable weld stress.
  8. Adopt a practical weld size and re-check.

6. Riveted Structural Joints

Riveted joints are permanent mechanical joints formed by rivets. For structural-joint design, understand the joint arrangement, load transfer and the principal failure modes of the connected plate and rivets.

Key failure ideas

  • Rivet shear
  • Rivet bearing/crushing
  • Plate tearing between or beside rivet holes
  • Joint efficiency when comparing a joint with the unperforated plate

For numerical design, use the exact dimensions, material values and design constants supplied in the question and the permitted data-book procedure.

7. Theory of Bonded Joints

A bonded joint uses an adhesive layer to transfer load between adherends. The behaviour depends on the adherend materials, adhesive properties, bond geometry, surface condition and load distribution.

Exam points

  • Identify adherend, adhesive layer and bonded area.
  • Load transfer occurs through the adhesive layer.
  • Surface preparation strongly influences joint performance.
  • Joint geometry should avoid unnecessary peel or concentrated stresses.
  • Consider service environment and compatibility of materials.

8. 12-Mark Answer — Eccentrically Loaded Bolted Joint

  1. Draw the joint and locate the bolt-group centroid.
  2. Resolve the applied load and determine the direct component.
  3. Calculate the eccentric moment.
  4. Determine moment-induced bolt loads according to bolt position relative to the centroid.
  5. Combine direct and moment-induced effects.
  6. Identify the most heavily loaded bolt.
  7. Check the bolt/joint against the applicable allowable condition.
  8. Adopt practical dimensions and re-check.
M = P × e

Conclusion: An eccentric joint must not be treated as a purely direct-load problem.

9. 12-Mark Answer — Knuckle Joint Design

  1. State function and tensile loading.
  2. Determine allowable stresses from the stated material and factor of safety.
  3. Design the rod diameter.
  4. Design/check the knuckle pin.
  5. Check the single eye for tearing and bearing.
  6. Check the fork for tearing, shear and bearing.
  7. Identify the governing dimension.
  8. Adopt practical dimensions and verify all sections.

Conclusion: Final dimensions are governed by the most critical failure condition after all required checks.

10. 12-Mark Answer — Cotter Joint Design

  1. State the axial load and materials.
  2. Calculate allowable tensile, shear and crushing stresses as applicable.
  3. Design the rod.
  4. Determine cotter dimensions from shear and bearing requirements.
  5. Check the weakened rod/spigot section.
  6. Check socket and collar sections.
  7. Check the cotter itself.
  8. Adopt practical dimensions and re-check every governing condition.

Conclusion: A complete cotter-joint answer is a sequence of strength checks, not a single diameter calculation.

11. 12-Mark Answer — Welded Joint under Eccentric Loading

  1. Draw the weld group, load and eccentricity.
  2. Locate the weld-group centroid.
  3. Calculate direct stress/shear due to the applied load.
  4. Calculate the eccentric moment.
  5. Determine the moment-induced stress distribution.
  6. Locate the critical weld point.
  7. Combine the relevant stresses.
  8. Compare the maximum resultant stress with the allowable value.
  9. Adopt weld size/length and perform the final check.

Diagram requirement: Show load, eccentricity, centroid, weld dimensions and critical point clearly.

12. 12-Mark Answer — Riveted Structural Joint

  1. Draw the riveted joint and identify the load path.
  2. State rivet and plate dimensions/material data.
  3. Check rivet shear.
  4. Check rivet bearing/crushing.
  5. Check plate tearing at the critical section.
  6. Determine the governing joint strength.
  7. If required, calculate joint efficiency.
  8. Adopt practical dimensions and state the governing failure mode.

13. 12-Mark Answer — Theory of Bonded Joints

  1. Define a bonded joint and identify adherends and adhesive.
  2. Explain load transfer through the adhesive layer.
  3. Discuss the effect of bond area and joint geometry.
  4. Explain the importance of surface preparation.
  5. Discuss common stress concentrations and peel effects.
  6. Explain material compatibility and service-environment considerations.
  7. State advantages and limitations.
  8. Conclude with appropriate design considerations.

16. Numerical Discipline

  1. Given: write every value with units.
  2. To find: state the required dimension/stress.
  3. Formula: write the governing equation before substitution.
  4. Substitute: show actual numerical values.
  5. Intermediate result: do not skip important arithmetic.
  6. Adopt: select a practical/data-book dimension when required.
  7. Re-check: calculate the actual stress for the adopted dimension.
  8. Final: state the answer and whether the design is safe.
