1. Overhang Crank – Q11(a)

Given:

P = 1 kN = 1000 N; Syt = 400 N/mm²; FOS = 2; vertical arm = 500 mm; eccentricity e = 50 + 100 + 25 = 175 mm. Maximum shear stress theory.

To find:

Diameter at section XX.

Solution:

Step 1: Allowable shear stress

Maximum shear stress theoryτallow=Syt2×FOS

τallow = 400/(2 × 2) = 100 N/mm².

Step 2: Loading analysis

The load produces bending about XX because of the 500 mm arm and torsion about the shaft axis because of the 175 mm eccentricity. Therefore, both M and T must be considered.

Step 3: Bending moment

Bending momentM=P×L

M = 1000 × 500 = 500 × 10³ Nmm.

Step 4: Torque

TorqueT=P×e

T = 1000 × 175 = 175 × 10³ Nmm.

Step 5: Stresses in the solid circular section

Bending and torsional stressesσb=32Mπd3;τt=16Tπd3

Step 6: Apply maximum shear stress theory

Maximum shear stressτmax=σb22+τt2

100 = √[(σb/2)² + τt²].

Substituting σb = 32(500 × 10³)/(πd³) and τt = 16(175 × 10³)/(πd³), and solving gives d ≈ 29.8 mm.

Step 7: Check and selection

Choose d ≥ 29.8 mm. Adopt d = 30 mm and verify that τmax does not exceed 100 N/mm².

Result: Diameter at section XX = 30 mm.

2. Fatigue Plate – Q11(b)

Given:

45C8 plate; Sut = 630 N/mm²; completely reversed axial load = ±50 kN; reliability = 90%; FOS = 2; size factor = 0.85; notch radius = 5 mm; minimum width b = 50 mm.

To find:

Thickness t for infinite fatigue life.

Solution:

Step 1: Unmodified endurance limit

Unmodified endurance limitS′e=Sut2

S′e = 630/2 = 315 N/mm².

Step 2: Fatigue factors

Ka = 0.76, Kb = 0.85, Kc = 0.897, Kd = 0.496.

Kt = 2.27 and q = 0.8.

Fatigue stress-concentration factorKf=1+q(Kt−1)

Kf = 1 + 0.8(2.27 − 1) = 2.016.

Step 3: Corrected endurance limit

Corrected endurance limitSe=KaKbKcKdS′e

Se = 0.76 × 0.85 × 0.897 × 0.496 × 315 = 90.54 N/mm².

Step 4: Alternating design stress

For the design-data treatment used for this problem, the conservative alternating design stress is based on 0.8Se/FOS.

Allowable alternating stressσa=0.8SeFOS

σa = (0.8 × 90.54)/2 = 36.216 N/mm².

Step 5: Calculate plate thickness

Axial stress at the minimum sectionσa=Pbt

36.216 = 50000/(50t).

36.216 × 50t = 50000.

t = 50000/(36.216 × 50) = 27.61 mm.

Step 6: Check

Adopt t ≥ 27.61 mm. Hence choose t = 28 mm.

Result: Plate thickness = 28 mm.

3. Square Key – Q12(a)

Given:

Shaft diameter d = 25 mm; P = 15 kW = 15000 W; N = 720 rpm; key material 50C4; Syt = 460 N/mm²; FOS = 3.

To find:

Suitable square-key dimensions.

Solution:

Step 1: Transmitted torque

Power–torque relationT=60P2πN

T = [60 × 15000/(2π × 720)] × 10³ = 198944 Nmm.

Step 2: Allowable stresses

Allowable shear stressτallow=Syt2×FOS

τallow = 460/(2 × 3) = 76.67 N/mm².

σc,allow = 460/3 = 153.33 N/mm².

Step 3: Initial key dimensions

For a square key, take b = t and use b ≈ d/4 = 25/4 = 6.25 mm as the theoretical starting value.

Step 4: Length from shear

Key shear conditionT=τallowbld2

l = T/[τallow b(d/2)].

l = 198944/[76.67 × 6.25 × 12.5] = 33.2 mm.

Step 5: Crushing check

Key crushing conditionT=σc,allowt2ld2

The theoretical square key is checked for crushing using σc,allow = 153.33 N/mm² before final selection.

Step 6: Select practical dimensions

Round upward to a practical standard size and recheck both modes. Adopt b = 7 mm, t = 7 mm and l = 35 mm.

Result: Square key = 7 mm × 7 mm × 35 mm.

4. Muff Coupling – Q12(b)

Given:

P = 25 kW = 25000 W; N = 360 rpm; shaft 30C8 with Syt = 400 N/mm²; sleeve FG200 with Sut = 200 N/mm²; FOS = 4 for shaft/key and 6 for sleeve.

To find:

Shaft diameter, sleeve dimensions and key dimensions.

Solution:

Step 1: Transmitted torque

Power–torque relationT=60P2πN

T = [60 × 25000/(2π × 360)] × 10³ = 663146 Nmm.

Step 2: Design shaft

Allowable shaft shear stressτallow=Syt2×FOS

τallow = 400/(2 × 4) = 50 N/mm².

Solid shaft in torsionT=π16τallowd3

d = [16T/(πτallow)]1/3.

d = [16 × 663146/(π × 50)]1/3 = 40.73 mm.

Select the next suitable standard diameter: d = 45 mm.

Step 3: Sleeve diameter

Using the applicable sleeve proportion, D ≈ 2d + 13 = 2(45) + 13 = 103 mm. Adopt D = 105 mm.

Step 4: Sleeve length

L ≈ 3.5d = 3.5(45) = 157.5 mm. Adopt L = 160 mm.

Step 5: Sleeve strength check

Allowable sleeve shear = 200/(2 × 6) = 16.67 N/mm².

Sleeve torsional stressτ=16TDπ(D4−d4)

τ = 16(663146)(105)/[π(105⁴ − 45⁴)] ≈ 3.02 N/mm².

3.02 N/mm² < 16.67 N/mm², so the sleeve is safe.

Step 6: Key design and checks

For a 45 mm shaft, use the applicable PSG standard key size b × t = 14 × 9 mm. Take l = 80 mm.

Key shear stressτ=2Tdbl

τ = 2(663146)/(45 × 14 × 80) = 26.32 N/mm² < 50 N/mm².

Key crushing stressσc=4Tdtl

σc = 4(663146)/(45 × 9 × 80) = 81.87 N/mm² < 100 N/mm².

Result: Shaft d = 45 mm; sleeve D = 105 mm, L = 160 mm; key = 14 mm × 9 mm × 80 mm.

5. Double-Threaded ISO Metric Trapezoidal Power Screw – Q13(a)

Given:

W = 300 kN = 300000 N; nominal diameter d = 100 mm; pitch p = 12 mm; double thread; μ = 0.15; collar friction neglected.

To find:

Torque to raise the load, torque to lower the load and efficiency.

Solution:

Step 1: Mean diameter and lead

For the stated ISO metric trapezoidal thread, d2 = 94 mm.

Lead of a double-threaded screwL=2p

L = 2 × 12 = 24 mm.

Step 2: Equivalent friction coefficient

Equivalent friction coefficientμ′=μ sec β

β = 15°.

μ′ = 0.15 sec15° = 0.15529.

Step 3: Helix angle

Helix angletan α=Lπd2

α = tan⁻¹[24/(π × 94)] = 4.646°.

Step 4: Friction angle

Friction angleφ=tan−1μ′

φ = tan⁻¹(0.15529) = 8.827°.

Step 5: Torque to raise the load

Raising torqueTR=Wd22 tan(φ+α)

TR = (300000 × 94/2) tan(8.827° + 4.646°).

TR ≈ 3.378 × 10⁶ Nmm = 3378 N·m.

Step 6: Torque to lower the load

Lowering torqueTL=Wd22 tan(φ−α)

TL = (300000 × 94/2) tan(8.827° − 4.646°) ≈ 1.031 × 10⁶ Nmm = 1031 N·m.

Step 7: Efficiency

Screw efficiencyη=WL2πTR×100

η = [300000 × 24/(2π × 3.378 × 10⁶)] × 100 ≈ 33.9%.

Since φ > α, the screw is self-locking for the stated conditions.

Result: Raising torque ≈ 3378 N·m; lowering torque ≈ 1031 N·m; efficiency ≈ 33.9%.

6. Rectangular Fillet Weld – Q13(b)

Given:

P = 25 kN = 25000 N; eccentricity e = 500 mm; weld rectangle = 150 mm × 100 mm; permissible weld shear = 75 N/mm².

To find:

Fillet weld size s.

Solution:

Step 1: Total weld length

L = 2(150 + 100) = 500 mm.

Step 2: Throat thickness

Fillet-weld throatt=0.707s

Step 3: Direct shear

Direct weld shearτd=P0.707sL

τd = 25000/(0.707 × s × 500) = 70.72/s N/mm².

Step 4: Eccentric moment

Moment due to eccentric loadM=Pe

M = 25000 × 500 = 12.5 × 10⁶ Nmm.

Step 5: Weld-group section modulus

For the stated rectangular weld group, the applicable section property gives Z ≈ 15907.5s mm³.

Moment-induced weld stressτm=MZ

τm = 12.5 × 10⁶/(15907.5s) = 785.8/s N/mm².

Step 6: Combine stresses

Maximum shear conditionτmax=12σb2+4τd2

Putting the permissible shear stress equal to 75 N/mm² and solving gives s ≈ 5.32 mm.

Step 7: Selection and check

Select the next practical weld size above 5.32 mm: s = 6 mm.

Result: Fillet weld size = 6 mm.

7. Hydrodynamic Journal Bearing – Q14(b)

Given:

W = 3.2 kN = 3200 N; N = 1490 rpm; d = 50 mm; L = 50 mm; radial clearance c = 0.05 mm; lubricant viscosity = 25 cP.

To find:

Coefficient of friction, power loss, minimum film thickness, oil flow rate and temperature rise.

Solution:

Step 1: Bearing pressure

Bearing pressurep=WLd

p = 3200/(50 × 50) = 1.28 N/mm² = 1.28 MPa.

Step 2: Bearing geometry

L/d = 50/50 = 1.

Using the applicable hydrodynamic-bearing convention, the Sommerfeld number for the stated conditions is approximately 0.121.

Step 3: Read the bearing chart

For L/d = 1 and Sommerfeld number ≈ 0.121, the applicable Raimondi–Boyd/PSG chart gives approximately f = 0.00644 and hmin/c = 0.40.

Step 4: Minimum film thickness

Minimum film thicknesshmin=0.40c

hmin = 0.40 × 0.05 = 0.020 mm = 20 μm.

Step 5: Journal surface speed

Surface speedv=πdN60

Using d = 0.05 m, v = π × 0.05 × 1490/60 = 3.901 m/s.

Step 6: Power loss

Friction power lossP=fWv

P = 0.00644 × 3200 × 3.901 = 80.4 W.

Step 7: Oil flow

From the applicable bearing chart for the same operating condition, total oil flow is approximately Q = 0.403 L/min.

Step 8: Temperature rise

Assume the generated heat is carried away by the total oil flow, as stated in the question. Take lubricant density ρ ≈ 860 kg/m³ and specific heat Cp ≈ 1760 J/(kg·°C).

Heat balanceP=m˙CpΔT

Q = 0.403 L/min = 0.403 × 10⁻³/60 = 6.717 × 10⁻⁶ m³/s.

ṁ = ρQ = 860 × 6.717 × 10⁻⁶ = 0.00578 kg/s.

ΔT = 80.4/(0.00578 × 1760) ≈ 7.9°C.

The calculated film thickness is positive and the heat balance is consistent with the question's stated assumption.

Result: f ≈ 0.00644; power loss ≈ 80.4 W; hmin ≈ 0.020 mm; oil flow ≈ 0.403 L/min; temperature rise ≈ 7.9°C.

8. Line Shaft with Two Pulleys – Q16(a)

Given:

Belt tension ratio = 3:1; limiting tight tension = 2.7 kN = 2700 N; shaft 40C8; Sut = 650 N/mm²; Syt = 380 N/mm²; keyed pulleys; Kb = 1.5; Kt = 1.0. Distances: C–A = 450 mm, A–B = 450 mm, B–D = 250 mm. Pulley A diameter = 250 mm; pulley B diameter = 450 mm.

To find:

Critical bending moment and shaft diameter.

Solution:

Step 1: Belt tensions at pulley A

T1 = 2700 N.

T1/T2 = 3 ⇒ T2 = 2700/3 = 900 N.

Step 2: Torque transmitted by pulley A

Torque from belt tensionsT=(T1−T2)D2

T = (2700 − 900)(250/2) = 225000 Nmm.

Step 3: Belt tensions at pulley B

The same torque is transmitted at B:

(T3 − T4)(450/2) = 225000.

T3 − T4 = 1000 N.

T3/T4 = 3 ⇒ T4 = 500 N, T3 = 1500 N.

Step 4: Vertical reactions

Vertical belt load at A = T1 + T2 = 3600 N.

Taking moments about C: RDv(1150) = 3600(450).

RDv = 1408.7 N.

RCv = 3600 − 1408.7 = 2191.3 N.

Step 5: Horizontal reactions

Horizontal belt load at B = T3 + T4 = 2000 N.

Taking moments about C: RDh(1150) = 2000(900).

RDh = 1565.2 N.

RCh = 2000 − 1565.2 = 434.8 N.

Step 6: Bending moment at A

Mv,A = RCv(450) = 2191.3 × 450 = 986087 Nmm.

Mh,A = RCh(450) = 434.8 × 450 = 195652 Nmm.

Resultant bending momentMb=Mv2+Mh2

Mb,A = √(986087² + 195652²) ≈ 1.005 × 10⁶ Nmm.

The corresponding resultant at B is approximately 526 × 10³ Nmm, so A is critical.

Step 7: ASME allowable stress

ASME allowable shear stressτallow=min(0.30Syt,0.18Sut)

τallow = min(0.30 × 380, 0.18 × 650) = min(114,117) = 114 N/mm².

For the keyed shaft, τallow = 0.75 × 114 = 85.5 N/mm².

Step 8: Combined bending and torsion

ASME shaft equationd3=16πτallowKbMb2+KtT2

d³ = [16/(π × 85.5)]√[(1.5 × 1.005 × 10⁶)² + (1.0 × 225000)²].

d ≈ 44.95 mm.

Step 9: Selection and check

Adopt d = 45 mm. The selected standard diameter is greater than the calculated diameter.

Result: Shaft diameter = 45 mm.

9. Socket/Cotter Joint – Q16(b)

Given:

Rod diameter d = 50 mm; cotter plate thickness = 15 mm; material 40C8; Syt = 380 N/mm²; compressive yield strength = 2Syt; shear yield strength = 0.5Syt; FOS = 6.

To find:

Socket dimensions.

Solution:

Step 1: Allowable tensile stress

Allowable tensile stressσt,allow=SytFOS

σt,allow = 380/6 = 63.33 N/mm².

Step 2: Allowable compressive stress

Allowable compressive stressσc,allow=2SytFOS

σc,allow = 760/6 = 126.67 N/mm².

Step 3: Allowable shear stress

Allowable shear stressτallow=0.5SytFOS

τallow = (0.5 × 380)/6 = 31.67 N/mm².

Step 4: Load in the rod

Tensile load in rodP=π4d2σt,allow

P = (π/4)(50²)(63.33) = 124348 N ≈ 124.35 kN.

Step 5: Socket failure checks

Check the socket for tensile failure at the net section, shear failure around the cotter, crushing/bearing at the cotter surfaces and the corresponding joint-end failure modes. Using the applicable socket/cotter-joint equations with cotter thickness 15 mm gives the theoretical dimensions:

Inside socket diameter d2 = 60.45 mm.

Outside socket diameter d1 = 84.21 mm.

Collar diameter d3 = 130.44 mm.

a = 28.04 mm.

c = 30.20 mm.

Step 6: Round upward and check

The theoretical dimensions are rounded upward to practical values so that the adopted dimensions do not reduce the calculated strength.

d2 = 65 mm; d1 = 85 mm; d3 = 135 mm; a = 30 mm; c = 35 mm.

Result: Adopt d₂ = 65 mm, d₁ = 85 mm, d₃ = 135 mm, a = 30 mm and c = 35 mm.

Fixed ACADRIX numerical-writing rule

  1. Given: write all numerical data with units.
  2. To find: state exactly what is required.
  3. Solution: explain the physical concept and analyse the relevant section or part.
  4. Calculate intermediate quantities one at a time.
  5. Box only the important governing formulas.
  6. Write fractions with numerator above denominator.
  7. Show substitutions and intermediate calculations clearly.
  8. Perform the required unit, stress, strength or design check.
  9. Round theoretical dimensions upward to suitable practical/standard values.
  10. Write the final result plainly — never box the answer.
