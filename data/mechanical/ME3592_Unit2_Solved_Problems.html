ME3592 — Unit II Solved Problems

Measurement of Linear, Angular Dimensions, Assembly and Transmission Elements • R-2021

Evidence: The worked examples on this page are instructional practice unless explicitly labelled as a verified university question. They are not to be treated as PYQs merely because the topic appears frequently in examinations.

Problem 1 — Sine Bar: Find the Angle

Given: Distance between roller centres, L = 200 mm. Slip-gauge height, h = 50 mm.

Find: Angle θ set by the sine bar.

Step 1 — Governing relation

sin θ = hL

Step 2 — Substitute

sin θ = 50/200 = 0.25

Step 3 — Calculate

θ = sin⁻¹(0.25)

θ = 14.4775°

Step 4 — Convert to degrees and minutes

0.4775 × 60 = 28.65′

Therefore θ ≈ 14°28.65′ ≈ 14°29′.

Final answer: θ ≈ 14°29′.

Exam presentation: draw the two rollers, show L, h and θ, then write the relation before substitution.

Problem 2 — Sine Bar: Find Slip-Gauge Height

Given: Roller-centre distance L = 200 mm. Required angle θ = 30°.

Find: Slip-gauge height h.

Step 1 — Formula

h = L sin θ

Step 2 — Substitute

h = 200 × sin 30°

Step 3 — Calculate

sin 30° = 0.5

h = 200 × 0.5 = 100 mm

Final answer: Required slip-gauge height = 100 mm, neglecting practical corrections.

Problem 3 — Mean and Range of Repeated Measurements

Given: Five readings: 20.02, 20.04, 20.03, 20.01 and 20.05 mm.

Find: Mean and range.

Step 1 — Sum of readings

Σx = 20.02 + 20.04 + 20.03 + 20.01 + 20.05

Σx = 100.15 mm

Step 2 — Arithmetic mean

x̄ = Σxn

x̄ = 100.15/5 = 20.03 mm

Step 3 — Range

Range = xmax − xmin

Range = 20.05 − 20.01 = 0.04 mm

Final answer: Mean = 20.03 mm; range = 0.04 mm.

Problem 4 — Comparator Deviation

Given: A comparator is zeroed using a 25.000 mm standard. The workpiece produces a positive deviation of +0.012 mm.

Find: Workpiece dimension.

Step 1 — Measurement principle

The comparator reading represents the deviation from the preset reference.

Step 2 — Apply deviation

Workpiece size = Standard size + Deviation

= 25.000 + 0.012

= 25.012 mm

Final answer: Workpiece dimension = 25.012 mm.

A comparator is normally used for comparison rather than as a direct absolute measuring instrument.

Problem 5 — Comparator Magnification

Given: A comparator produces an indicated movement of 12 mm for an actual workpiece deviation of 0.03 mm.

Find: Magnification.

Step 1 — Formula

Magnification = Indicated movementActual input movement

Step 2 — Substitute

Magnification = 12/0.03

Step 3 — Calculate

Magnification = 400

Final answer: Comparator magnification = 400.

Problem 6 — Gauge-Block Combination: Method

Task: Explain how to construct a gauge-block combination for a specified dimension.

  1. Write the required dimension to the available resolution.
  2. Start with the least significant digit.
  3. Select a block that removes that digit while leaving a remainder that can be formed by available blocks.
  4. Continue until the required dimension is obtained.
  5. Use as few blocks as practicable.
  6. Verify the arithmetic sum before wringing.
Exam point: Show the selected block values and their sum; do not simply state “select suitable blocks.”

Problem 7 — Angular Measurement: Sine-Bar Error Awareness

Question: List the quantities that can affect the accuracy of a sine-bar setting.

  • Roller-centre distance accuracy
  • Slip-gauge dimensional accuracy
  • Surface flatness and parallelism
  • Workpiece contact and deformation
  • Alignment of the workpiece
  • Cleanliness and stable seating
Exam conclusion: The ideal relation h = L sin θ assumes accurate geometry and stable contact; practical errors must be controlled.

Important Unit II Numerical Practice

  1. Vernier caliper: calculate a reading from a supplied scale diagram.
  2. Micrometer: calculate a reading including zero correction when supplied.
  3. Sine bar: calculate angle from slip-gauge height.
  4. Sine bar: calculate slip-gauge height for a specified angle.
  5. Comparator: calculate deviation from a standard.
  6. Comparator: calculate magnification.
  7. Three-wire method: determine effective diameter using the specified thread relation.
  8. Gear measurement: interpret runout, pitch variation, tooth thickness or lead data.

Numerical discipline

  1. Given — write every value with units.
  2. Find — state exactly what is required.
  3. Formula — write the governing equation.
  4. Substitute — insert the actual values.
  5. Arithmetic — show intermediate calculations.
  6. Final answer — include units and suitable rounding.

For IS 919 fits/gauges and detailed thread/gear calculations, use the relevant standard tables or data supplied by the question. Do not invent catalogue or table values.
