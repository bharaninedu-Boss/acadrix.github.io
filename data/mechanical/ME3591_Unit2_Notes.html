1. Unit II syllabus map

Unit II covers shafts and axles; solid and hollow shafts based on strength, rigidity and critical speed; keys and splines; rigid couplings; and flexible couplings.

2. Shafts and axles

A shaft is a member used to transmit power and/or support rotating elements. A transmission shaft commonly experiences bending and torsion. An axle is generally used to support rotating components and is mainly subjected to bending.

Design checks include strength, torsional rigidity and lateral rigidity.

3. Torque from transmitted power

T = 9.55 × 10³ P / N
T in N·m when P is in kW and N in rpm.
T = 9.55 × 10⁶ P / N
T in N·mm when P is in kW and N in rpm.

4. Shaft stresses

σb = 32M/(πd³)
τ = 16T/(πd³)

When bending and torsion act together, use the failure theory specified in the question.

5. Combined bending and torsion

Te = √(M² + T²)
Equivalent twisting moment for the maximum-shear-stress approach.
Me = 1/2 [M + √(M² + T²)]
Equivalent bending moment for the maximum-principal-stress approach.

6. Design stress

Ssd = min(0.18Sut, 0.30Syt)

Apply the correction/service factors stated in the problem; do not invent them.

7. Shaft rigidity

θ = TL/(GJ)
J = πd⁴/32

Critical speed is important when rotating masses produce lateral vibration and resonance. The operating speed should be kept safely away from the critical speed.

9. Forces acting on shafts

For belt drives, the difference in belt tensions transmits torque and the belt pulls create bending load. For spur gears, tangential force transmits torque and radial force produces bending.

FR = FT tan ψ

10. Keys and splines

A key connects a hub to a shaft and transmits torque. Check both shear and crushing.

T = τallowbld/2
T = σc,allowhld/4

Splines are multiple integral keys that provide higher torque capacity and can guide a hub. Know parallel-sided and involute spline types and their terminology.

11. Couplings

A coupling joins two shafts and transmits torque. Rigid couplings require good alignment; flexible couplings accommodate permitted misalignment and help absorb shock.

12. Muff, flange and flexible couplings

For a sleeve/muff coupling, check the sleeve as a hollow shaft and check the key for shear and crushing. For flange couplings, check shaft, key, bolts, hub and flange. Flexible bushed-pin couplings additionally require checks on the pins and bushes.

13. 12-Mark Answer — Design of a Shaft under Bending and Torsion

  1. Draw the shaft, bearings and applied loads.
  2. Resolve gear/belt forces into appropriate planes.
  3. Calculate bearing reactions.
  4. Draw bending-moment diagrams and identify the critical section.
  5. Calculate transmitted torque from power and speed.
  6. Determine the maximum bending moment and torque at the critical section.
  7. Select the specified failure theory and obtain the equivalent moment.
  8. Calculate the required shaft diameter from allowable design stress.
  9. Check torsional rigidity and lateral rigidity.
  10. Check critical speed where required.
  11. Adopt a practical standard diameter and re-check.

Conclusion: Final shaft size is governed by the most restrictive applicable strength, rigidity and speed requirement.

14. 12-Mark Answer — Design of a Key

  1. Determine the shaft torque.
  2. Select standard key width and thickness from the permitted data.
  3. Assume or determine key length from the design requirement.
  4. Check key shear.
  5. Check key crushing.
  6. Increase length if either condition is not satisfied.
  7. Choose a practical standard length and verify both conditions again.
T = τallowbld/2
T = σc,allowhld/4

Conclusion: Key length must satisfy both shear and crushing requirements.

15. 12-Mark Answer — Selection of Coupling

  1. State the shaft power and speed.
  2. Calculate transmitted torque.
  3. Identify whether rigid or flexible coupling is appropriate.
  4. Consider alignment, permitted misalignment, shock and maintenance requirements.
  5. Select a suitable coupling arrangement.
  6. Design/check shaft and key.
  7. For flange coupling, check bolts, hub and flange.
  8. For flexible bushed-pin coupling, check pins and bushes.
  9. Select standard dimensions and verify torque capacity.

Conclusion: Coupling selection depends on torque, shaft size, alignment, service conditions and the required flexibility.
