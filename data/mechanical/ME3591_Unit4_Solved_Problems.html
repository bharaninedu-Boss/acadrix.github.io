Example A — Helical Compression Spring

Notebook data: maximum load ≈ 450 N; deflection ≈ 12.5 mm; mean coil diameter ≈ 37.5 mm; working/allowable shear stress ≈ 300 N/mm²; G ≈ 0.8 × 105 N/mm².

τ = Kw 8PD/(πd³)

The handwritten trial calculation temporarily takes Kw = 1 and obtains d ≈ 5.2 mm. It then estimates the spring index and approximately 4 active coils, with approximately 6 total coils for square-and-ground ends.

Exam check: The notebook's d ≈ 5.2 mm is a classroom trial result. For a final design, calculate Kw from C = D/d and re-check both stress and deflection before adopting the dimension.

Example B — Helical Spring Stress & Deflection

Notebook data: outside diameter Do = 80 mm, wire diameter d = 12 mm, load P = 1 kN, number of coils ≈ 105, G ≈ 0.89 × 105 N/mm².

D = Do − d = 80 − 12 = 68 mm
C = D/d = 68/12 ≈ 5.67
Kw = (4C−1)/(4C−4) + 0.615/C
τ = Kw 8PD/(πd³)
δ = 8PD³N/(Gd⁴)

Keep force in N, dimensions in mm and G in N/mm² so that stress is in N/mm² and deflection is in mm.

1. April/May 2024 Q14(a) — Helical Compression Spring

Verified source problem: The supplied April/May 2024 answer document identifies this as Q14(a). The question gives maximum load 1250 N, deflection about 30 mm, spring index 6, patented and cold-drawn steel wire, Sut = 1090 N/mm² and G = 81370 N/mm². fileciteturn619file0

To find: wire diameter, mean coil diameter, active coils, total coils, free length and pitch.

Step 1 — Permissible shear stress

τallow = 0.5Sut
τallow = 0.5 × 1090 = 545 N/mm²

Step 2 — Wahl factor

K = 4C − 14C − 4 + 0.615C
C = 6
K = (24 − 1)/(24 − 4) + 0.615/6
K = 23/20 + 0.1025
K = 1.2525

Step 3 — Strength-only trial diameter

τ = 8W D Kπd³

Since D = Cd:

τ = 8WCKπd²
545 = [8 × 1250 × 6 × 1.2525]/(πd²)
d² = 7,515,000/(1712.38)
d² ≈ 4,387
d ≈ 66.2 mm

The displayed source answer uses the final practical design d = 7 mm. The strength-only rearrangement above exposes an inconsistency in the supplied worked solution if the equation is interpreted literally with d in mm. Therefore ACADRIX does not silently claim this intermediate calculation proves the final 7 mm size. The final design values below are reproduced from the supplied answer document.

Step 4 — Final adopted spring geometry from the supplied solution

d = 7 mm
D = Cd = 6 × 7 = 42 mm
Do = D + d = 49 mm
Di = D − d = 35 mm

Step 5 — Spring stiffness

k = Wδ
k = 1250/30 = 41.667 N/mm

Step 6 — Active coils

k = Gd⁴8D³N
N = Gd⁴/(8D³k)
N = (81370 × 7⁴)/(8 × 42³ × 41.667)
N ≈ 8

Step 7 — Total coils and solid length

Nt = N + 2 = 10 coils
Ls = Ntd = 10 × 7 = 70 mm

Step 8 — Actual deflection check

δ = 8WD³NGd⁴
δ = [8 × 1250 × 42³ × 8]/[81370 × 7⁴]
δ ≈ 30.34 mm
30.34 mm ≈ specified 30 mm → deflection requirement is satisfied.

Step 9 — Free length

Total axial gap = 9 × 1 = 9 mm
Lf = Ls + δ + gap
= 70 + 30.34 + 9
= 109.34 mm
Adopt Lf ≈ 110 mm

Step 10 — Pitch

p = LfNt − 1
p = 109.34/(10 − 1)
p = 109.34/9
p = 12.15 mm

Final reproduced design: d = 7 mm, D = 42 mm, Do = 49 mm, Di = 35 mm, N = 8, Nt = 10, Lf ≈ 110 mm, p ≈ 12.15 mm, actual δ ≈ 30.34 mm. fileciteturn619file13

2. April/May 2024 Q14(b) — Flywheel Disk

Verified source problem: The supplied answer document gives a motor with constant torque, resisting torque increasing uniformly from 500 to 1500 N·m through one revolution, mean angular velocity 30 rad/s, coefficient of speed fluctuation 0.2, a solid circular steel disk of thickness 25 mm, density 7800 kg/m³ and Poisson's ratio 0.3. fileciteturn619file2

To find: outer radius and maximum stresses in the disk.

Step 1 — Coefficient of fluctuation of speed

Cs = ωmax − ωminω
ωmax − ωmin = Csω = 0.2 × 30 = 6 rad/s

Step 2 — Maximum fluctuation of energy

The mean torque equals the average resisting torque:

Tmean = (500 + 1500)/2 = 1000 N·m

The torque difference from the mean is 500 N·m. Over the half-cycle where torque differs from the mean, the maximum energy fluctuation is obtained from the area of the torque-angle diagram:

ΔE = 500 × π = 1570.8 N·m = 1570.8 J

Step 3 — Required moment of inertia

ΔE = Iω²Cs
I = ΔE/(ω²Cs)
= 1570.8/(30² × 0.2)
= 1570.8/180
= 8.7267 kg·m²

Step 4 — Relate inertia to solid disk radius

I = 12MR²
M = ρπR²t
I = 0.5(ρπR²t)R²
I = 0.5ρπtR⁴
R⁴ = 2I/(ρπt)
= 2(8.7267)/(7800 × π × 0.025)
≈ 0.02849 m⁴
R ≈ 0.4106 m

Step 5 — Stress equations for a rotating solid disk

For the adopted solid-disk model, use the standard rotating-disk radial and tangential stress relations with the material density, Poisson ratio and angular velocity. The maximum value occurs according to the radial position specified by those relations.

Verification note: The supplied document gives the complete problem statement, but the searchable excerpt available here does not expose enough of its stress-calculation pages to reproduce the exact final stress values without guessing. ACADRIX therefore stops at the verified radius calculation rather than inventing the missing stress result.

3. Connecting Rod — Full Numerical Method

Generated study method: This is not a PYQ because no complete numerical dataset is available in the verified source excerpt.
  1. Calculate gas force from piston area and cylinder pressure.
  2. Calculate reciprocating inertia force for the specified crank position.
  3. Find the net axial load.
  4. Select the connecting-rod I-section.
  5. Calculate area and second moments of area.
  6. Determine radius of gyration in both planes.
  7. Apply the prescribed column/buckling relation.
  8. Check small end, big end and cap bolts where data are supplied.
  9. Re-check the critical section and state the governing condition.

4. Crankshaft — Full Numerical Method

Generated study method: Numerical structure only; not an official PYQ.
  1. Calculate gas force.
  2. Determine connecting-rod force and obliquity angle.
  3. Resolve radial and tangential components.
  4. Calculate torque from tangential force.
  5. Find bearing reactions.
  6. Construct bending moments in the relevant planes.
  7. Find the critical section.
  8. Design the journal/crank section for combined bending and torsion using the prescribed criterion.
  9. Check stress concentration/fatigue and practical proportions.

5. Exam Checklist

  • Do not skip units.
  • Show every substitution.
  • Do not confuse spring wire diameter with mean coil diameter.
  • For flywheels, first calculate energy fluctuation before sizing the disk.
  • For connecting rods, check buckling as well as direct loading.
  • For crankshafts, never use torque alone when bending loads are present.
  • Use the permitted PSG Design Data Book for standard dimensions and tabulated constants.
