1. April/May 2024 Q14(a) — Helical Compression Spring

Verified source problem: The supplied April/May 2024 answer document identifies this as Q14(a). The question gives maximum load 1250 N, deflection about 30 mm, spring index 6, patented and cold-drawn steel wire, Sut = 1090 N/mm² and G = 81370 N/mm². fileciteturn619file0

To find: wire diameter, mean coil diameter, active coils, total coils, free length and pitch.

Step 1 — Permissible shear stress

τallow = 0.5Sut
τallow = 0.5 × 1090 = 545 N/mm²

Step 2 — Wahl factor

K = 4C − 14C − 4 + 0.615C
C = 6
K = (24 − 1)/(24 − 4) + 0.615/6
K = 23/20 + 0.1025
K = 1.2525

Step 3 — Strength-only trial diameter

τ = 8W D Kπd³

Since D = Cd:

τ = 8WCKπd²
545 = [8 × 1250 × 6 × 1.2525]/(πd²)
d² = 7,515,000/(1712.38)
d² ≈ 4,387
d ≈ 66.2 mm

The displayed source answer uses the final practical design d = 7 mm. The strength-only rearrangement above exposes an inconsistency in the supplied worked solution if the equation is interpreted literally with d in mm. Therefore ACADRIX does not silently claim this intermediate calculation proves the final 7 mm size. The final design values below are reproduced from the supplied answer document.

Step 4 — Final adopted spring geometry from the supplied solution

d = 7 mm
D = Cd = 6 × 7 = 42 mm
Do = D + d = 49 mm
Di = D − d = 35 mm

Step 5 — Spring stiffness

k = Wδ
k = 1250/30 = 41.667 N/mm

Step 6 — Active coils

k = Gd⁴8D³N
N = Gd⁴/(8D³k)
N = (81370 × 7⁴)/(8 × 42³ × 41.667)
N ≈ 8

Step 7 — Total coils and solid length

Nt = N + 2 = 10 coils
Ls = Ntd = 10 × 7 = 70 mm

Step 8 — Actual deflection check

δ = 8WD³NGd⁴

Total axial gap = 9 × 1 = 9 mm
Lf = Ls + δ + gap
= 70 + 30.34 + 9

p = LfNt − 1
p = 109.34/(10 − 1)
p = 109.34/9

2. April/May 2024 Q14(b) — Flywheel Disk

Verified source problem: The supplied answer document gives a motor with constant torque, resisting torque increasing uniformly from 500 to 1500 N·m through one revolution, mean angular velocity 30 rad/s, coefficient of speed fluctuation 0.2, a solid circular steel disk of thickness 25 mm, density 7800 kg/m³ and Poisson's ratio 0.3. fileciteturn619file2

To find: outer radius and maximum stresses in the disk.

Step 1 — Coefficient of fluctuation of speed

Cs = ωmax − ωminω
ωmax − ωmin = Csω = 0.2 × 30 = 6 rad/s

Step 2 — Maximum fluctuation of energy

The mean torque equals the average resisting torque:

Tmean = (500 + 1500)/2 = 1000 N·m

The torque difference from the mean is 500 N·m. Over the half-cycle where torque differs from the mean, the maximum energy fluctuation is obtained from the area of the torque-angle diagram:

ΔE = 500 × π = 1570.8 N·m = 1570.8 J

Step 3 — Required moment of inertia

ΔE = Iω²Cs
I = ΔE/(ω²Cs)
= 1570.8/(30² × 0.2)
= 1570.8/180
= 8.7267 kg·m²

Step 4 — Relate inertia to solid disk radius

I = 12MR²
M = ρπR²t
I = 0.5(ρπR²t)R²
I = 0.5ρπtR⁴
R⁴ = 2I/(ρπt)
= 2(8.7267)/(7800 × π × 0.025)
≈ 0.02849 m⁴
R ≈ 0.4106 m

Step 5 — Stress equations for a rotating solid disk

For the adopted solid-disk model, use the standard rotating-disk radial and tangential stress relations with the material density, Poisson ratio and angular velocity. The maximum value occurs according to the radial position specified by those relations.

Problem 1 — Helical Compression Spring Design

Source: This worked example is based on the handwritten Unit IV pages you supplied. It is classroom/notebook material, not a university PYQ.

QuantityValue
Maximum load, P450 N
Required deflection, δ12.5 mm
Mean coil diameter, D37.5 mm
Working shear stress300 N/mm²
Modulus of rigidity, G0.8 × 105 N/mm²

Step 1 — Write the stress equation

For a close-coiled helical compression spring:

τ = Kw × 8PD/(πd³)

The handwritten solution first takes Kw = 1 as a trial calculation.

Step 2 — Calculate the trial wire diameter

300 = (8 × 450 × 37.5)/(πd³)

Rearrange:

d³ = (8 × 450 × 37.5)/(300π)
d³ ≈ 143.24 mm³
d ≈ 5.23 mm

Hence the notebook obtains approximately d = 5.2 mm as the trial wire diameter.

Step 3 — Calculate the spring index

C = D/d
C = 37.5/5.2 ≈ 7.21

So the spring index is approximately C = 7.2.

Step 4 — Recalculate the Wahl factor

Kw = (4C − 1)/(4C − 4) + 0.615/C
Kw = (4×7.21 − 1)/(4×7.21 − 4) + 0.615/7.21
Kw ≈ 1.206

Step 5 — Check the actual corrected shear stress

τ = 1.206 × [8×450×37.5/(π×5.2³)]
τ ≈ 368.6 N/mm²

Important: 368.6 N/mm² is higher than the stated 300 N/mm² working stress. Therefore 5.2 mm should not be accepted as the final wire diameter if the 300 N/mm² limit is the governing requirement. The notebook's 5.2 mm is a trial result obtained before the Wahl correction.

Step 6 — Determine a corrected wire diameter

Because D = 37.5 mm is fixed in the notebook example, try a slightly larger wire diameter and recalculate C and Kw. For example, with d = 5.6 mm:

C = 37.5/5.6 = 6.696
Kw ≈ 1.224
τ ≈ 293.6 N/mm²

Thus a practical selection near 5.6 mm satisfies the 300 N/mm² stress condition for this fixed-D interpretation. The exact adopted standard wire size should follow the design-data table/instruction used in your class.

Step 7 — Calculate active coils for the corrected trial d = 5.6 mm

δ = 8PD³N/(Gd⁴)

Rearrange:

N = δGd⁴/(8PD³)
N = [12.5×(0.8×105)×5.6⁴]/[8×450×37.5³]
N ≈ 5.05 active coils

Adopt the number of active coils according to the specified end condition and design convention, then re-check deflection.

Step 8 — Total coils

For the notebook's square and ground end condition, the notes use:

Nt = N + 2

Therefore, after adopting the final active-coil value, add the appropriate inactive end coils and use the resulting total coil count for solid length.

Step 9 — Solid length

Ls = Ntd

Substitute the adopted total coil count and final wire diameter.

Step 10 — Free length

Lf = Ls + δ + allowance

The notebook explicitly writes free length as solid length + maximum deflection + allowance. Do not invent the allowance value if the question does not specify it; use the prescribed design-data rule.

Step 11 — Pitch

p = (Lf − d)/(Nt − 1)

Use the adopted free length, wire diameter and total number of coils. Present the pitch in mm.

Exam conclusion: The handwritten 5.2 mm diameter is a useful trial calculation, but once the Wahl factor is included the stress becomes about 368.6 N/mm². Therefore, show the correction and re-check before accepting the final design.

Problem 2 — Helical Spring: Maximum Shear Stress and Deflection

Notebook data: outside diameter Do = 80 mm, wire diameter d = 12 mm, axial load P = 1 kN, number of coils N = 105, and G = 0.89 × 105 N/mm².

Step 1 — Convert the load

P = 1 kN = 1000 N

Step 2 — Calculate mean coil diameter

Do = D + d
D = Do − d = 80 − 12 = 68 mm

Mean coil diameter, D = 68 mm.

Step 3 — Calculate spring index

C = D/d = 68/12 = 5.667

Step 4 — Calculate Wahl stress factor

Kw = (4C − 1)/(4C − 4) + 0.615/C
Kw = (4×5.667 − 1)/(4×5.667 − 4) + 0.615/5.667
Kw ≈ 1.269

Step 5 — Calculate maximum shear stress

τmax = Kw × 8PD/(πd³)
τmax = 1.269 × [8×1000×68/(π×12³)]
τmax ≈ 127.2 N/mm²

Maximum shear stress ≈ 127.2 MPa.

Step 6 — Calculate total deflection

δ = 8PD³N/(Gd⁴)
δ = [8×1000×68³×105]/[(0.89×105)×12⁴]
δ ≈ 143.12 mm

Total deflection ≈ 143.1 mm.

Step 7 — Calculate spring stiffness, if required

k = P/δ = 1000/143.12 ≈ 6.99 N/mm

Step 8 — Factor of safety

Factor of safety requires a specified allowable shear stress, yield strength or ultimate shear strength.

FOS = τallow/τmax

The supplied notebook image does not provide a sufficiently clear strength/allowable-stress value for a defensible numerical FOS. Therefore do not invent one. If the question supplies τallow, divide it by 127.2 MPa.

Final verified calculations: D = 68 mm; C = 5.667; Kw ≈ 1.269; τmax ≈ 127.2 MPa; δ ≈ 143.1 mm; k ≈ 6.99 N/mm. FOS requires the missing strength/allowable-stress value.

📝 How to Present Spring Numericals in the Exam

  1. Write all given values first and convert kN → N.
  2. Identify whether the given diameter is outside, inside or mean diameter.
  3. Calculate mean diameter before using spring-index equations.
  4. Calculate spring index C = D/d.
  5. Calculate Wahl factor whenever corrected maximum shear stress is required.
  6. Calculate stress and compare it with the allowable value.
  7. Use the deflection equation to find active coils or check deflection.
  8. Find total coils based on the specified end condition.
  9. Find solid length, free length and pitch.
  10. State the final adopted dimensions in one boxed line.
