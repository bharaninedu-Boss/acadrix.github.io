Nov/Dec 2023 — Question Paper Code 21302

Year: 2023 Units I–V

PART A — 2-Mark Answers

Q1. Summarize various elements of measurement. Unit I
A measurement system consists of the measurand, standard/reference, sensing or input element, conversion/transmission element, signal conditioning and indicating/recording element. The system compares the unknown quantity with a known standard and produces a usable indication.
Q2. Mention the main objectives of traceability. Unit I
Traceability links a measurement result to recognized standards through an unbroken documented chain of calibrations. Its objectives are to establish confidence in measurement results, maintain consistency between laboratories, support calibration validity and provide a known uncertainty.
Q3. List some parameters measured by angle gauges. Unit II
Angle gauges are used to build/verify precise angles. They can be used for checking angular dimensions, setting sine-bar/sine-table arrangements, checking taper angles and calibrating or setting angular measuring equipment.
Q4. Discuss the working principle of an autocollimator. Unit II
An autocollimator sends a collimated light beam to a reflecting surface. The reflected image returns through the optical system. A small angular tilt of the reflector produces a proportional displacement of the image at the focal plane. The displacement is converted into angular measurement.
Q5. Define tolerance and mention its classification. Unit III
Tolerance is the permissible variation in a specified dimension, equal to the difference between upper and lower limits. Common classifications include unilateral, bilateral and compound tolerance.
Q6. Draw the relationship between fundamental, upper and lower deviations in limits and fits. Unit III
The zero line represents the basic size. Upper deviation is the algebraic difference between maximum limit and basic size; lower deviation is the algebraic difference between minimum limit and basic size. The tolerance zone lies between these deviations.
Q7. What is the purpose of angle dekkor? Unit II
An angle dekkor is an optical angular measuring instrument used for precise measurement/checking of small angular deviations, slopes and tapers. It is useful for comparing an unknown angle with a reference setting.
Q8. Explain radial runout and axial runout. Unit IV
Radial runout is the variation of a surface's radial position during one revolution about the datum axis. Axial runout is the variation of an end/face surface in the axial direction during rotation. Both are commonly checked using a dial indicator.
Q9. Discuss the major applications of CMM. Unit V
CMMs measure dimensions, locations, form and orientation of complex features; inspect profiles and free-form surfaces; verify CAD geometry; inspect dies, moulds, gears and precision components; and support computer-aided inspection and reverse engineering.
Q10. Outline the elements of a flexible inspection system. Unit V
A flexible inspection system typically includes sensing/measurement hardware, a programmable controller or computer, fixture/workholding, data acquisition and processing, inspection software, CAD/nominal data, communication interfaces and result/reporting facilities.

PART B — 13-Mark Answers

Q11(a). Elaborate two broad categories of errors in measurement with suitable example. Unit I

Measurement errors are commonly classified into systematic and random errors.

Systematic errors repeat in a predictable manner. Sources include instrument calibration error, zero error, environmental effects, loading and observational bias. Example: a scale with a constant +0.02 mm zero error shifts every reading. They can be reduced by calibration, correction, environmental control and suitable measurement procedure.

Random errors vary unpredictably from reading to reading due to small uncontrolled effects such as vibration, electrical noise and reading fluctuations. Example: repeated readings of a dimension may be 20.01, 20.00, 20.02 mm. They are treated using repeated observations, mean/standard deviation and uncertainty estimation.

Conclusion: systematic effects require identification and correction; random effects are reduced statistically and represented through uncertainty.

Q11(b). Explain the working principle of a pneumatic back-pressure gauge. Discuss the relevance of the characteristic curve in measurement. Unit I

A pneumatic back-pressure gauge measures dimensional variation through the change in air back pressure produced when air flows through a controlled orifice toward the workpiece. The workpiece restricts the air escape path; a change in clearance changes the back pressure. The pressure indication is therefore related to dimensional variation.

The characteristic curve plots instrument indication (pressure or differential pressure) against the dimensional gap. Its useful near-linear region gives sensitivity and working range. Calibration establishes the relation between indication and size; the curve also helps identify non-linearity and the suitable operating zone.

Q12(a). Discuss the procedure to measure unknown angles with a sine bar. Also mention sine blocks, sine plates and sine tables. Unit II

For a sine bar of centre distance L, place one roller on a slip-gauge stack of height h and the other on the datum surface. The workpiece is supported on the sine bar and adjusted until the reference surface is level/parallel as required. Then:

sin θ = h/L    ⇒    θ = sin⁻¹(h/L)

Build h with calibrated slip gauges. Check contact, cleanliness and alignment before taking the final setting.

Sine block: compact sine device for setting/checking angles, often used with gauge blocks.
Sine plate: sine mechanism integrated with a plate for setting workpieces at a precise angle.
Sine table: larger sine device used for holding/setting workpieces and inspection setups.

Q12(b). Outline the constructional details of gear tooth caliper to measure tooth thickness. Unit II

A gear tooth vernier/caliper has two perpendicular slide systems. One sets the chordal addendum (depth) and the other measures the chordal tooth thickness at that depth. The vertical blade is first set to the required chordal height; the horizontal jaws are then brought into contact with the two flanks of the tooth.

For a standard gear, the instrument reading is compared with the theoretical chordal tooth thickness. The method is useful for checking tooth thickness without measuring over several teeth.

Q13(a). Clearance fit for a 40 mm shaft/bearing assembly: hole tolerance 0.006 mm, shaft tolerance 0.004 mm, unilateral, allowance 0.002 mm. Find limits for hole-basis and shaft-basis systems. Unit III

For a clearance fit, minimum clearance = allowance = 0.002 mm. Total clearance variation equals the sum of hole and shaft tolerances:

Cmax = 0.002 + 0.006 + 0.004 = 0.012 mm

(i) Hole-basis system: take the minimum hole as 40.000 mm and hole upper limit as 40.006 mm. To obtain minimum clearance 0.002 mm, the shaft maximum must be 39.998 mm. With 0.004 mm shaft tolerance, shaft minimum is 39.994 mm.

Q13(b). Summarize methods of tolerance specification on inspection gauges with diagrams of disposition. Unit III

Gauge tolerance is assigned around the basic gauge size so that the gauge itself has a controlled manufacturing tolerance. A common approach is to allocate a small fraction of the work tolerance to gauge manufacture. For GO gauges, wear allowance is considered because the GO gauge is used frequently.

For a hole, the GO plug gauge checks the minimum material condition and the NO-GO plug checks the maximum material condition. For a shaft, GO and NO-GO snap/ring gauges similarly check the corresponding limits. A tolerance-zone diagram should show the workpiece limits, gauge tolerance zones and wear allowance relative to the zero/basic reference.

Q14(a). Explain the working principle of a stylus-type surface roughness tester with neat sketch. Unit IV

A stylus tester drags a fine stylus over the surface at a controlled speed. Vertical stylus movement follows the surface profile and is converted by a pickup/transducer into an electrical signal. The signal is amplified, filtered according to the selected cutoff and processed to obtain roughness parameters such as Ra and Rz.

Blocks: stylus → pickup/transducer → amplifier → filtering/evaluation → display/recording. The cutoff length separates roughness from longer-wavelength waviness/profile components.

Q14(b). Discuss acceptance tests for a milling machine with required illustration. Unit IV

Acceptance testing checks whether a machine tool meets specified geometric and functional accuracy. Typical checks include table flatness, straightness and parallelism of travel, spindle alignment, squareness between axes, spindle runout, table movement accuracy, backlash, repeatability and performance under load/cutting conditions.

Dial indicators, precision levels, straightedges, test mandrels and reference artefacts are commonly used. The measured deviations are compared with the specified acceptance tolerances. The report records setup, reference direction, measured error and permissible error.

Q15(a). Elaborate the construction and working principle of a laser interferometer used in metrology. Unit V

A laser interferometer uses a highly coherent laser beam and optical elements such as beam splitters and reflectors. The beam is divided into reference and measurement paths; after reflection, the beams recombine and form interference. Movement of the measurement reflector changes optical path length and causes fringe/count or phase change.

For a double-pass displacement measurement, displacement d = Nλ/2

where N is the counted fringe change and λ is the laser wavelength, subject to the instrument's optical configuration and environmental compensation. Laser interferometers are used for displacement, straightness, angular error, alignment and machine-tool calibration.

Q15(b). Analyze five popular physical configurations used in machine vision systems. Unit V

Common camera/object arrangements include fixed camera–moving object, moving camera–fixed object, multiple-camera, line-scan and area-scan configurations. Selection depends on object motion, field of view, required resolution and inspection speed.

Lighting, lens, camera trigger and image-processing hardware/software are selected as a complete system. A stable geometry and repeatable illumination are essential for reliable inspection.

Q17(b). Enunciate the stages involved in a machine vision system with an example. Unit V

Typical stages: illumination → image formation by lens/camera → image acquisition → preprocessing/enhancement → segmentation → feature extraction → classification/decision → output/control and data storage.

Example: automated inspection of a machined component: controlled lighting exposes the profile, a camera acquires the image, preprocessing removes noise, segmentation isolates the component, dimensional/shape features are extracted and the result is compared with tolerance limits. The controller then accepts or rejects the component.

Apr/May 2024 — Question Paper Code 51345

Year: 2024 Units I–V

PART A — 2-Mark Answers

Q1. State any three factors affecting measurements. Unit I
Environmental conditions (temperature, humidity, vibration), instrument characteristics/calibration and operator/observational effects are three major factors. Workpiece condition and measurement method also influence results.
Q2. Briefly enumerate calibration of measuring instruments. Unit I
Calibration compares the instrument with a traceable standard at specified points, determines deviations/corrections and records uncertainty. If required, adjustment is made and the instrument is rechecked; the calibration result is documented.
Q3. Write the various types of linear measuring instruments. Unit II
Steel rule, vernier caliper, micrometer, height gauge, depth gauge, bore gauge, gauge blocks/slip gauges and comparators are common linear measurement devices.
Q4. Write any four precautions when using slip gauges. Unit II
Clean the blocks and contact faces; remove burrs; use wringing correctly; avoid excessive force; protect blocks from rust and scratches; use gloves/clean hands where appropriate; return blocks to their case after use.
Q5. What is interchangeability? Unit III
Interchangeability is the ability of mating parts manufactured within specified limits to assemble and perform their function without selective fitting or individual adjustment.
Q6. Define tolerance stack-up. Unit III
Tolerance stack-up is the accumulated effect of individual dimensional tolerances on a functional dimension or assembly condition. It may be evaluated by worst-case or statistical methods such as RSS.
Q7. Distinguish conventional and geometric tolerance. Unit III
Conventional dimensional tolerance controls size variation. Geometric tolerance controls form, orientation, location or runout relative to specified datums/conditions.
Q8. What factors affect surface roughness? Unit IV
Cutting speed, feed, depth of cut, tool geometry/wear, work material, machine-tool rigidity, vibration/chatter, cutting fluid and process type affect surface roughness.
Q9. How to calibrate the interferometer? Unit V
Use a traceable displacement/reference standard and verify the optical setup and zero. Compare known displacement with indicated displacement over the working range, determine correction/error and document the calibration and uncertainty. Environmental compensation is important for laser interferometers.
Q10. Mention applications of machine vision system. Unit V
Automated inspection, dimensional checking, surface-defect detection, identification/reading, robot guidance, assembly verification, sorting and process monitoring.

PART B — 13-Mark Answers

Q11(a). Explain in detail the classification of various measuring methods. Unit I

Measurement methods may be classified as:

  • Direct: measurand is read directly from an instrument scale/display, e.g. vernier caliper.
  • Indirect: measurand is calculated from other measured quantities, e.g. angle from sine-bar height.
  • Absolute: measurement is related directly to fundamental quantities/standards.
  • Comparative: unknown is compared with a standard or master, e.g. comparator.
  • Contact: sensing element physically contacts the workpiece.
  • Non-contact: optical/laser/vision methods avoid physical contact.
  • Deflection: measured quantity produces a deflection proportional to it.
  • Null: system is balanced against a reference until the indication is zero/minimum.

The selection depends on accuracy, range, speed, accessibility, surface condition and measurement uncertainty.

Q11(b). Elucidate systematic and random errors in measurements. Unit I
Systematic errors have identifiable direction/magnitude or repeatable behaviour and arise from calibration, zero, environmental, loading and method effects. They are minimized by calibration, correction and controlled conditions. Random errors fluctuate unpredictably; repeated observations, statistical analysis and averaging reduce their effect. Standard deviation and uncertainty quantify remaining variability.
Q12(a). Explain the working principle of micro-optic autocollimator. Unit II

An autocollimator contains a light source, illuminated reticle, collimating objective and viewing/detection system. The objective forms a parallel beam from the reticle. A reflector returns the beam. When the reflector is normal to the optical axis, the returned image coincides with the reticle image. Angular tilt shifts the returned image in the focal plane.

Image displacement y ≈ 2fθ

for small angle θ in the standard autocollimator arrangement, where f is focal length. The factor 2 arises because the light path experiences the reflector tilt on the outgoing and returning paths.

Q12(b). Explain sine bar and bevel protractor with neat sketches. Unit II

Sine bar: precision bar with two equal rollers separated by known centre distance L. A slip-gauge stack of height h sets the angle: sinθ=h/L. It is used for accurate angle setting and measurement.

Bevel protractor: consists of a graduated circular scale, stock/blade and adjustable blade. The blade is aligned with the two surfaces whose angle is required, and the scale reading gives the included angle. It is faster than a sine-bar setup but generally has lower precision.

Q13(a). Explain interchangeable manufacture and interchangeable assembly with examples. Unit III

Interchangeable manufacture means producing mating components within specified tolerance zones so any component can be used with any corresponding component. Interchangeable assembly means assembling such parts without individual fitting or selective matching.

Example: standardized bearings, bolts/nuts or mass-produced shafts and bushes manufactured to specified fits.

Benefits include mass production, simplified assembly, reduced inspection/repair time and easy replacement.

Q13(b). Shaft limits 30.02 mm and 29.98 mm; find bush limits for maximum clearance 0.10 mm and minimum clearance 0.02 mm. Unit III

Shaft maximum = 30.02 mm; shaft minimum = 29.98 mm.

Minimum clearance occurs with smallest bush and largest shaft:

Cmin = Bushmin − 30.02 = 0.02
Bushmin = 30.04 mm

Maximum clearance occurs with largest bush and smallest shaft:

Cmax = Bushmax − 29.98 = 0.10
Bushmax = 30.08 mm

Therefore the bush limits are 30.04 to 30.08 mm, giving 0.02 to 0.10 mm clearance.

Q14(a). Enlist and explain methods for measuring roundness. Unit IV
Common methods include V-block method, three-point/three-lobe related methods, rotational roundness tester, Talyrond-type instrument and dial-indicator comparison. In a rotational tester, the workpiece or spindle rotates about a reference axis while a high-resolution probe senses radial variation; the polar profile is recorded and roundness error is evaluated using a suitable reference circle.
Q14(b). Explain optical measurement techniques and 3D surface metrology. Unit IV

Optical measurement uses light without physical contact. Examples include projection/profile measurement, interferometry, laser scanning, structured light and machine vision. Advantages include non-contact measurement, high speed and suitability for delicate/complex surfaces.

3D surface metrology captures a three-dimensional surface topography rather than a single line profile. It can provide areal parameters, form information, texture maps and defect characterization. Optical 3D methods are useful for complex free-form surfaces and rapid inspection.

Q15(a). Explain AC laser interferometer and straightness measurement. Unit V

A laser interferometer generates a coherent reference/measurement beam pair. For straightness measurement, the optical setup is arranged so that lateral displacement of the moving target changes the relative optical path or beam angle. The interferometer detects the resulting fringe/phase change and converts it to displacement/angular information.

The guideway is traversed in increments; measured deviations are tabulated and a reference line is constructed to determine straightness error. Environmental compensation is used when required.

Q15(b). Explain types of CMM based on construction and advantages of computer-aided inspection. Unit V

Common CMM configurations include bridge, cantilever, gantry and horizontal-arm types. The choice depends on workpiece size, accessibility, rigidity and accuracy requirements.

Computer-aided inspection provides automatic coordinate acquisition, CAD comparison, programmable inspection plans, repeatability, digital reports, statistical data and integration with manufacturing/quality systems. It reduces manual calculation and supports complex 3D inspection.

Q16(b). Electronic caliper: five readings 21.53, 21.51, 20.52, 21.48, 21.42 mm; calibration ±0.02 mm; k=2. Estimate expanded uncertainty. Unit I

Mean of the five supplied readings:

x̄ = (21.53+21.51+20.52+21.48+21.42)/5 = 21.292 mm

Using the five readings as supplied, the sample standard deviation is approximately s=0.4336 mm. Type-A standard uncertainty:

uA=s/√n=0.4336/√5≈0.1939 mm

The calibration certificate states ±0.02 mm. Treating the limit as a rectangular distribution gives:

uB=0.02/√3≈0.01155 mm

Combined standard uncertainty:

uc=√(uA²+uB²)≈0.1942 mm

With coverage factor k=2:

U≈0.3885 mm ≈ 0.39 mm

Thus the result can be reported as approximately 21.29 ± 0.39 mm (k=2), using the supplied readings without rejecting any observation. The 20.52 mm reading is visibly separated from the others, but the paper does not provide an outlier-rejection rule, so it is not silently discarded.

Nov/Dec 2024 — Question Paper Code 41382

Year: 2024 Units I–V

PART A — 2-Mark Answers

Q1. Differentiate between sensitivity and accuracy. Unit I
Sensitivity is the change in instrument output per unit change in input. Accuracy is the closeness of the measured value to the true/reference value. A highly sensitive instrument is not necessarily accurate.
Q2. Define calibration of a measuring instrument. Unit I
Calibration is the documented comparison of an instrument's indication with a traceable reference under specified conditions, establishing errors/corrections and measurement uncertainty.
Q3. List four angular measuring instruments and rank based on accuracy. Unit II
Examples are bevel protractor, sine bar, angle gauges and autocollimator. Accuracy depends on instrument class, setup and application; in precision angular inspection, autocollimator/angle-gauge based methods are generally used for finer angular work than an ordinary bevel protractor. A fixed universal ranking should not be assumed without the specified instrument grades.
Q4. List two opto-mechanical measurement devices with an application. Unit II
Examples: autocollimator for small angular/straightness/alignment measurements; optical comparator/profile projector for checking profiles, dimensions and angles without contact.
Q5. What is the advantage of interchangeability? Unit III
It permits assembly and replacement of standardized parts without individual fitting, supporting mass production, lower assembly time, easier maintenance and reduced manufacturing/inspection cost.
Q6. Draw an example schematic for tolerance callout as per standard. Unit III
A dimensional tolerance may be shown as 50.00 ± 0.02 mm (bilateral) or as limit dimensions such as 50.02 / 49.98 mm. A GD&T callout uses a feature-control frame containing the geometric symbol, tolerance value and datum references as applicable.
Q7. Infer the importance of GD&T. Unit III
GD&T specifies allowable geometric variation of form, orientation, location and runout in a functional, standardized way. It improves design communication, manufacturability, inspection consistency and assembly control.
Q8. Write the advantage of 3D surface metrology. Unit IV
It measures areal surface topography and provides a more complete representation than a single profile. It supports free-form surface characterization, defect detection and areal roughness/texture analysis.
Q9. Why is laser preferred in engineering metrology? Unit V
Laser light is highly coherent, directional and monochromatic, enabling precise interferometry and long-distance non-contact measurement. It supports high resolution and repeatable displacement/alignment measurements.
Q10. List benefits of CMM for measurement. Unit V
High coordinate accuracy, automated multi-feature inspection, CAD comparison, complex 3D geometry measurement, repeatable programs, digital reporting and reduced manual measurement/calculation.

PART B — 13-Mark Answers

Q11(a). Elaborate different types of errors in measurement and remedies. Unit I

Systematic errors: instrumental, environmental, observational and method/loading errors that exhibit predictable behaviour. Remedies include calibration, zero correction, temperature control, vibration isolation, proper alignment and standardized procedure.

Random errors: unpredictable fluctuations. Remedies include repeated readings, averaging, statistical analysis and uncertainty evaluation.

Gross errors: mistakes such as wrong scale reading, incorrect setup or recording. They are reduced through training, repeat checks, controlled procedures and independent verification.

Q11(b). What is uncertainty in measurement? Explain types and estimation methods. Unit I

Measurement uncertainty quantifies the dispersion reasonably attributable to the measurand result. Type A uncertainty is evaluated statistically from repeated observations. Type B uncertainty is evaluated from calibration certificates, specifications, resolution, prior data and other information.

For independent components:

uc = √(u1²+u2²+…)

Expanded uncertainty is U=kuc, where k is the coverage factor. The result should state the estimate, uncertainty, coverage factor and relevant conditions.

Q12(a). Detail the setup and functions of a pneumatic comparator with schematic application. Unit I

A pneumatic comparator uses regulated air supplied through a nozzle/restricted passage. The gap between nozzle and workpiece controls back pressure or differential pressure. A pressure indicator converts this change to a dimensional indication.

Functions: comparative size measurement, rapid inspection, amplification of small dimensional variations, checking batches against a master and non-contact/low-force measurement.

A typical schematic is: compressed air → filter/regulator → restrictor → measuring nozzle → workpiece gap → pressure/differential indicator.

Q12(b). Explain Angle Dekkor construction, principle and measurement of V-block/taper-gauge slope. Unit II

An angle dekkor is an optical angular comparator consisting of an illuminated scale/reticle, collimating optics, reflecting arrangement and viewing microscope/eyepiece. The instrument converts small angular tilt into an image displacement on a calibrated scale.

For checking a V-block or taper, place the component in a stable reference arrangement, align the optical system with the reference surface, observe the reflected image, note the angular deviation, then repeat for the second face if the included angle is required. The difference between the measured surface inclinations gives the required included/slope angle.

Q13(a). Justify tolerance specification. Discuss unilateral, bilateral and compound tolerance. Unit III

Tolerances are necessary because exact dimensions cannot be manufactured economically. They establish functional limits while controlling manufacturing cost.

Unilateral: variation is permitted on one side of the basic dimension, e.g. 50 +0.02/−0.00 mm.
Bilateral: variation is permitted on both sides, e.g. 50 ±0.02 mm.
Compound: a functional dimension depends on multiple component dimensions whose individual tolerances combine into an overall tolerance/stack.

Q14(a). What is GD&T? Discuss form tolerances with schematics. Unit III

Geometric Dimensioning and Tolerancing is a symbolic system for defining allowable geometric variation. Form tolerances control the shape of individual features without requiring a datum.

  • Straightness: controls a line element or derived median line.
  • Flatness: confines a surface between two parallel planes separated by the tolerance.
  • Circularity: confines each circular cross-section between two concentric circles.
  • Cylindricity: confines the entire cylindrical surface between two coaxial cylinders.

In an exam, draw the corresponding feature-control symbols and tolerance zones beside the component surface.

Q14(b). Describe construction and measurement methodology of a stylus-type surface roughness instrument. Unit IV
The stylus traverses the surface at a controlled speed; the pickup converts vertical movement to an electrical signal; amplification and filtering separate roughness from waviness/profile components; the processor calculates parameters such as Ra/Rq/Rz. Before measurement, select cutoff/evaluation length, clean the surface, align the traverse direction correctly and ensure stable contact. Record the selected filter, cutoff and evaluation length with the result.
Q15(a). Explain construction, functional features and working of an interferometer. Unit V

An interferometer consists of a coherent light source, beam splitter, reference and measurement paths, reflectors and a detector/observation system. The beam splitter creates two optical paths which recombine to produce interference. A path change shifts the interference fringes.

For a double-pass displacement arrangement, d=Nλ/2. Interferometers are used for precise displacement, flatness, straightness, wavelength/reference measurement and calibration of machine tools and precision stages.

Q15(b). Explain principle, basic functions and application of machine vision system with block diagram. Unit V

Block diagram: object/lighting → lens → camera/image sensor → image acquisition → preprocessing → segmentation/feature extraction → classification/measurement → decision/output.

The system converts optical information into digital image data and applies image-processing algorithms to measure or classify the object. Applications include dimensional inspection, defect detection, sorting, identification and robot guidance.

PART C — 15-Mark Answers

Q16(a). Differentiate major, minor and effective diameter of a screw thread and explain common measurement techniques. Unit II

Major diameter: largest diameter of an external thread (crest-to-crest) or corresponding largest diameter of an internal thread reference. Minor diameter: smallest diameter at the roots. Effective/pitch diameter: diameter of an imaginary cylinder where thread thickness equals space width under the specified measurement condition.

Common techniques include outside micrometer for major diameter, three-wire method for effective diameter of external threads, thread micrometer, optical/profile measurement and suitable gauges for checking functional fit. The three-wire method uses wires of known diameter placed in the thread grooves and measures over the wires; the effective diameter is calculated using the thread geometry and measured dimension.

Q16(b). Develop a method for straightness inspection of a machine-tool guideway using an autocollimator and tabulation. Unit II

Place a precision reflector/mirror on the moving carriage and align the autocollimator with the reference direction. Move the carriage through equal intervals and record the angular readings. Convert each angular reading θ to a linear deviation over the chosen base length L:

deviation ≈ Lθ   (θ in radians)

For the requested reference line, subtract the straight line joining the first and last measured points from the measured profile. The maximum positive/negative departure from this reference line gives the straightness error according to the chosen plane and sign convention.
