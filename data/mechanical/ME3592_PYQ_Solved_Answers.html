2023 • Q1 • Unit ISummarize various elements of measurement.
A measurement system consists of the measurand, standard/reference, sensing element, transducer or detector, signal conditioning/display system and the comparison/decision process. The measurement result is affected by the instrument, operator, environment, method and workpiece.
2023 • Q2 • Unit IMention the main objectives of traceability.
Traceability provides an unbroken documented chain of calibrations linking a measurement result to recognized standards. Objectives: establish confidence in measurements, maintain calibration validity, enable comparison between laboratories/industries, support quality systems and identify measurement uncertainty.
2023 • Q3 • Unit IIList some parameters measured by angle gauges.
Angle gauges are used to build or compare precise angles. Important parameters/applications include component angles, taper angles, angular fixture settings, machine-tool angular settings and other precision angular features.
2023 • Q4 • Unit IIDiscuss the working principle of an autocollimator.
An autocollimator projects a collimated beam onto a reflecting surface. The reflected beam returns through the optical system; a small angular tilt causes a proportional displacement of the image at the detector/eyepiece. Thus small angular deviations can be measured accurately and used for alignment/straightness checks.
2023 • Q5 • Unit IIIDefine tolerance and mention its classification.
Tolerance is the permissible variation in a specified dimension. It is commonly classified as unilateral when variation is permitted on one side of the basic size, and bilateral when variation is permitted on both sides.
2023 • Q6 • Unit IIIDraw the relationship between fundamental, upper and lower deviations in limits and fits.
Use the zero line as the basic-size reference. Upper deviation = upper limit − basic size; lower deviation = lower limit − basic size. The distance between the upper and lower deviation is the tolerance. In the exam, draw a horizontal zero line, mark the tolerance zone above/below it and label ES, EI, es and ei for hole and shaft.
2023 • Q7 • Unit IIWhat is the purpose of angle dekkor?
An angle dekkor is an optical instrument used to measure or compare small angular deviations. It is useful for checking small slopes, tapers and angular errors in precision components.
2023 • Q8 • Unit IVExplain radial runout and axial runout.
Radial runout is the variation of a rotating surface measured in a radial direction during one revolution. Axial runout is the variation of an end face measured parallel to the axis during rotation. Both indicate geometric error of a rotating component.
2023 • Q9 • Unit VDiscuss the major applications of CMM.
CMMs are used for dimensional inspection, feature location, profile/form checking, reverse engineering, inspection of complex components, first-off inspection, statistical quality control and comparison of measured geometry with CAD/design data.
2023 • Q10 • Unit VOutline the elements of a flexible inspection system.
A flexible inspection system typically includes sensor/probe, motion/positioning system, fixture, controller/computer, measurement software, data acquisition and analysis, and communication/storage. It is arranged so different components/features can be inspected with minimal setup change.

PART B — 5 × 13

2023 • Q11(a) • Unit IElaborate two broad categories of errors in measurement with suitable example.

The two broad categories are systematic errors and random errors.

Systematic errors: repeatable or predictable bias. Sources include calibration error, zero error, scale error, environmental effects and consistent observational effects. They can often be estimated and corrected. Example: a micrometer with a +0.02 mm zero error gives readings biased by approximately +0.02 mm.

Random errors: unpredictable variations that change in magnitude and/or sign between repeated measurements. Sources include vibration, electrical noise, small operator variation and uncontrolled environmental fluctuations. They are reduced statistically by repeated observations and suitable averaging.

Control: calibrate instruments, correct zero error, control temperature/vibration, use proper technique, repeat measurements and analyse the data statistically.

Conclusion: systematic error mainly affects measurement bias, while random error affects scatter/precision.

2023 • Q11(b) • Unit IExplain the working principle of a pneumatic back-pressure gauge. Discuss the relevance of the characteristic curve in measurement.

A pneumatic back-pressure gauge compares the air pressure developed in a restricted air circuit with a reference pressure. Compressed air passes through a control/restriction arrangement and exits through a nozzle. The gap between nozzle and workpiece changes the back pressure. That pressure change is converted into a readable displacement/pressure indication.

Exam sketch: draw the compressed-air source, restriction/control valve, nozzle, workpiece gap and pressure indication, followed by the characteristic curve of output versus gap/input.

Characteristic curve: it relates instrument output (such as back pressure) to input dimension/gap. The useful portion should be sufficiently sensitive and approximately linear. The curve helps determine sensitivity, working range, repeatability and suitable calibration points.

Advantages: high sensitivity for small dimensions, no mechanical contact in the measuring zone and suitability for rapid comparative inspection.

2023 • Q12(a) • Unit IIDiscuss the procedure to measure unknown angles with a sine bar. Mention sine blocks, sine plates and sine tables.

A sine bar uses the geometry of a right triangle formed by its roller centres. If the roller-centre distance is L and the required angle is θ, the height H is set so that:

sin θ = H / L   ⇒   H = L sin θ
  1. Clean the sine bar, surface plate and workpiece.
  2. Measure/select the roller-centre distance L.
  3. Place gauge blocks of calculated height H under one roller.
  4. Place the component on the sine bar and secure it carefully.
  5. Adjust/check the component until the reference surfaces are aligned as required.
  6. Calculate the unknown angle from sinθ = H/L.

Exam sketch: draw the sine bar with two rollers, gauge-block stack under one roller and the workpiece on the top surface. Label L and H.

Sine blocks: compact arrangements for setting/checking angles. Sine plate: sine bar mounted on a plate for supporting larger workpieces. Sine table: larger adjustable table used for setting precise angular positions, often for inspection or machining.

2023 • Q12(b) • Unit IIOutline the constructional details of a gear tooth caliper to measure tooth thickness.

Exam sketch: draw the perpendicular depth and tooth-thickness scales, vertical depth bar and horizontal measuring jaws, and label the chordal addendum/depth and tooth thickness.

Construction: A gear-tooth vernier caliper has two mutually perpendicular vernier arrangements. The vertical scale with depth bar sets the chordal addendum/depth, while the horizontal jaws and vernier measure the chordal tooth thickness.

Working: First set the vertical depth according to the specified gear geometry. Position the instrument on the tooth, bring the horizontal jaws into contact with the two tooth flanks at the required chordal position, and read the horizontal vernier. The depth and thickness settings are adjusted independently.

Use: It provides direct inspection of gear-tooth thickness without dismantling the gear and is useful for checking wear or manufacturing variation.

2023 • Q13(b) • Unit IIISummarize methods of tolerance specification on inspection gauges with diagrams of disposition.

Gauge dimensions are related to the workpiece limits so that the gauge checks functional acceptance. In the common general-purpose arrangement, the GO gauge checks the maximum-material condition and its tolerance zone is disposed within the work tolerance. The NO-GO gauge checks the opposite limit and is prevented from entering/passing when the workpiece is within the correct limit.

Methods: (1) unilateral gauge tolerance disposition, (2) bilateral disposition where specified, and (3) provision of wear allowance on GO gauges according to the adopted gauge-design standard.

In an exam diagram, draw the work tolerance zone first, then show GO and NO-GO gauge tolerance zones and the wear allowance in the correct direction.

2023 • Q14(a) • Unit IVExplain the working principle of a stylus-type surface roughness tester with a neat sketch.

A stylus instrument uses a fine stylus that traverses the surface over a specified sampling length. Surface irregularities cause vertical stylus movement. A transducer converts this mechanical movement into an electrical signal, which is amplified, filtered and processed to obtain surface parameters.

Exam sketch: draw the stylus/pickup, amplifier, filter/cut-off, processing and display as a block diagram; show the stylus traversing the measured surface.

Block sequence:

Stylus → pickup/transducer → amplifier → filter/cut-off → processing/display

The instrument can determine parameters such as Ra and other profile parameters depending on the selected standard/filter. The stylus force and traverse conditions must be controlled to avoid damaging the surface and distorting the measurement.

2023 • Q14(b) • Unit IVDiscuss various acceptance tests for a milling machine with required illustration.

Purpose: Acceptance tests verify whether a milling machine satisfies specified geometric and functional accuracy before acceptance or after maintenance.

Typical tests:

  • straightness and flatness of table/guideways,
  • parallelism of table travel and other reference surfaces,
  • squareness/perpendicularity of machine movements,
  • spindle runout and spindle alignment,
  • table movement and backlash,
  • accuracy of feed/positioning movement.

Procedure: Select the appropriate reference surface and instrument such as a dial indicator, precision straightedge, test mandrel or spirit level; take readings at specified positions and compare the measured deviation with the applicable acceptance tolerance.

Exam illustration: draw the machine member being tested, the reference/straightedge or test mandrel, dial indicator and direction of movement, and label the measured error.

2023 • Q15(a) • Unit VElaborate the construction and working principle of a laser interferometer used in metrology.

A laser interferometer uses a stable laser source, beam-splitting/optical elements, reference and measurement paths, reflector/target and a photodetector/processing system. The laser beam is divided into paths; changes in optical path length cause a phase difference and interference variation.

Exam sketch: show laser source → beam splitter → reference path and measurement path → reflector → recombination/detector. Indicate the change in optical path that produces fringe/phase change.

Because wavelength is known and stable, displacement can be related to fringe/phase change. The system provides high-resolution non-contact measurement and is widely used for machine-tool positioning, straightness, angular and displacement calibration.

Advantages: high resolution, long measuring range, good repeatability and suitability for calibration. Environmental compensation may be required because air refractive index changes with temperature, pressure and humidity.

2023 • Q15(b) • Unit VAnalyze five popular physical configurations used in machine vision systems.

Common physical arrangements include backlighting, front/diffuse lighting, coaxial/telecentric-style illumination, structured lighting and line-scan arrangements. The exact configuration depends on whether the objective is silhouette measurement, surface inspection, dimensional measurement or 3D/profile acquisition.

  1. Backlight: silhouette/edge inspection.
  2. Front/diffuse: general surface and feature inspection.
  3. Coaxial/telecentric: precise dimensional/feature imaging with controlled perspective.
  4. Structured light: depth/profile/3D information.
  5. Line scan: continuous webs, sheets or moving surfaces.

  2. Image acquisition: camera/sensor captures the object.

Apr/May 2024 • Q11(a) • Unit IExplain classification of various measuring methods.

Answer: Measurement methods may be classified according to how the measurand is obtained. Direct measurement gives the value directly from an instrument scale/display, while indirect measurement determines it from related measured quantities. Absolute methods use fundamental relationships/standards; comparative methods compare the workpiece with a standard/reference. Other useful classifications include contact/non-contact and deflection/null methods.

Examples: micrometer for direct measurement; sine-bar method for indirect angle determination; comparator for comparative measurement; optical/laser method for non-contact measurement.

The choice depends on range, accuracy, speed, accessibility, surface condition and production requirements.

Apr/May 2024 • Q11(b) • Unit IElucidate systematic and random errors in measurements.
Systematic errors are repeatable/predictable biases caused by calibration, zero, scale, environment or method; they are estimated and corrected where possible. Random errors vary unpredictably between observations and are reduced by repeated measurements and statistical analysis. Good practice includes calibration, environmental control, correct alignment, suitable instrument selection and statistical treatment.
Apr/May 2024 • Q12(a) • Unit IIWith neat sketch explain the working principle of micro-optic autocollimator.

Principle: A micro-optic autocollimator forms a collimated image using an optical source, objective and reticle arrangement. The beam is directed to a reflecting surface and returned through the optics. A small angular displacement of the reflector changes the image position at the focal plane; this displacement is proportional to the angular error.

Small angular error ∝ measured image displacement / optical scale factor

Applications include precision alignment, small-angle measurement, straightness and squareness checks. In the exam draw source/reticle → collimating optics → mirror/workpiece → reflected beam → detector/eyepiece.

Apr/May 2024 • Q12(b) • Unit IIExplain (i) sine bar (ii) bevel protractor.

Sine bar: a precision bar with two equal rollers. It generates an angle using gauge blocks: sinθ = H/L. It is used for accurate setting and measurement of angles.

Bevel protractor: an angular measuring instrument with a graduated circular scale, stock, blade and vernier in precision versions. The blade is aligned with the two surfaces and the angle is read from the scale/vernier.

Sine bars are generally used for higher-accuracy angle setting; bevel protractors are convenient for general angular measurement.

Apr/May 2024 • Q13(a) • Unit IIIExplain interchangeable manufacture and interchangeable assembly with examples.

Definition: Interchangeable manufacture means producing components within specified limits so that any acceptable component can replace another of the same designation. Interchangeable assembly means those components can be assembled without selective hand fitting and still satisfy the functional requirement.

Example: standardized bearing/shaft assemblies or mass-produced fasteners. Benefits include mass production, reduced assembly time, easier replacement and standardized inspection.

Apr/May 2024 • Q14(a) • Unit IVEnlist and explain methods for measuring roundness.

Methods of roundness measurement:

  1. V-block and indicator method: the workpiece is supported in a V-block, rotated, and radial variation is observed with an indicator.
  2. Rotating-spindle method: the component/pickup is associated with a precision rotary spindle and the radial error is recorded during rotation.
  3. Two-point/three-point comparison methods: selected contact points are used to compare dimensional variation as the component rotates.
  4. Dedicated roundness tester: a precision rotary table/spindle and pickup generate a polar error plot for quantitative evaluation.
  5. Optical methods: optical techniques can be used where contact is undesirable or profile information is required.

The method selected depends on required accuracy, component geometry, size and inspection purpose.

Apr/May 2024 • Q14(b) • Unit IVExplain optical measurement techniques and 3D surface metrology.

Optical measurement: Optical techniques use light/optics to measure dimensions, profiles or surface features without or with minimal contact. Examples include optical comparator/profile projector, microscope-based measurement, interferometry and structured-light methods.

3D surface metrology captures an areal surface rather than a single profile, allowing parameters related to height distribution, spatial structure and functional texture to be evaluated. It is useful for surfaces where directionality or local features cannot be represented adequately by a single 2D trace.

Apr/May 2024 • Q15(a) • Unit VExplain the working principle of AC laser interferometer and how straightness is measured.

Exam sketch: show the laser source, beam splitter, reference/measurement beams, reflector and detector. For straightness, show successive guideway positions and the reference line.

Working principle: A laser interferometer generates a stable optical reference and measurement beam. Motion of a reflector changes optical path length and hence the interference phase/fringe count. The measured displacement is obtained from the known laser wavelength and the observed phase/fringe change.

Straightness: mount the optical system along the reference direction, move the target/reflector along successive positions on the machine guideway and record the transverse/angular deviation. The deviations are processed relative to a chosen reference line to obtain the straightness error profile.

Apr/May 2024 • Q16(b) • Unit I • NumericalFive electronic-caliper readings: 21.53, 21.51, 20.52, 21.48, 21.42 mm. Calibration certificate: ±0.02 mm. Estimate expanded uncertainty, k=2.

Step 1 — Mean:

x̄ = (21.53+21.51+20.52+21.48+21.42)/5 = 21.292 mm

Step 2 — Type A standard uncertainty: using the sample standard deviation s = 0.4336 mm,

uA = s/√n = 0.4336/√5 = 0.1939 mm

Step 3 — Type B calibration uncertainty: treating ±0.02 mm as a rectangular distribution,

uB = 0.02/√3 = 0.01155 mm

Step 4 — Combined standard uncertainty:

uc = √(uA2 + uB2) = 0.1942 mm

Step 5 — Expanded uncertainty, k=2:

U = 2uc = 0.3885 mm ≈ 0.39 mm
Result: 21.29 ± 0.39 mm at approximately 95% coverage under the stated assumptions. The 20.52 mm reading produces the large Type A spread; it has not been silently discarded because the question gives it as a measurement.

Nov/Dec 2024 • Q11(a) • Unit IElaborate types of errors and remedies to minimize them.

Systematic errors: instrumental, environmental, observational and methodological biases. Remedies include calibration, correction curves, zero adjustment, temperature control, vibration isolation, proper alignment and standardized procedure.

Random errors: unpredictable scatter. Remedies include repeated measurements, averaging, statistical analysis and stable measurement conditions.

Gross errors: mistakes such as wrong reading, wrong setting or recording. Remedies include training, repeated checks, clear procedures and independent verification.

A good measurement system combines proper instrument selection, calibration, environmental control, operator training and statistical evaluation.

Nov/Dec 2024 • Q11(b) • Unit IExplain uncertainty in measurement, its types and estimation methods.

Measurement uncertainty quantifies the doubt associated with a measurement result. Type A evaluation is based on statistical analysis of repeated observations; Type B evaluation uses other information such as calibration certificates, specifications, previous data or assumed distributions.

For independent components:

uc = √(u12 + u22 + …)

Expanded uncertainty is:

U = k uc

where k is the coverage factor. For approximately 95% coverage, k≈2 is often used when the conditions support that approximation.

Nov/Dec 2024 • Q13(a) • Unit IIIJustify tolerance specification; explain unilateral, bilateral and compound tolerance.

Tolerances are necessary because manufacturing cannot achieve exact nominal dimensions. Proper tolerancing ensures function, interchangeability and economical production.

Unilateral: variation permitted mainly on one side of basic size, e.g. 25.00 +0.02/−0.00 mm.

Bilateral: variation permitted on both sides, e.g. 25.00 ±0.02 mm.

Compound tolerance: a tolerance scheme in which the allowable variation of a feature is controlled through more than one related requirement/dimension or combined tolerance condition; the drawing should make the functional relationship explicit.

Nov/Dec 2024 • Q14(a) • Unit IVExplain GD&T and different types of form tolerances.

Exam sketch: draw a feature-control frame and illustrate the four form-tolerance symbols: straightness, flatness, circularity and cylindricity, with their tolerance zones.

Definition: GD&T is a symbolic system for controlling geometric variation relative to size and datums. Form tolerances control the shape of an individual feature without requiring a datum.

  • Straightness: controls deviation from a straight line/axis or surface element.
  • Flatness: controls how much a surface may depart from an ideal plane.
  • Circularity (roundness): controls each circular element of a surface.
  • Cylindricity: controls the complete cylindrical surface in a 3D zone.

Draw the standard feature-control symbols and explain the tolerance zone for each.

Nov/Dec 2024 • Q14(b) • Unit IVDescribe construction and measurement methodology of a stylus surface roughness instrument.

Exam sketch: draw stylus → pickup/transducer → amplifier → filter/evaluation → display, with the stylus traversing the surface.

The instrument has a stylus, pickup/transducer, traverse mechanism, amplifier, filtering/evaluation system and display. The stylus traverses the surface over a specified length; vertical movement is converted to an electrical signal. Filtering separates roughness from longer-wavelength form/waviness components, after which surface parameters are calculated.

Important controls include sampling length/cut-off, traverse speed, stylus force, filter/evaluation standard and surface cleanliness.

Nov/Dec 2024 • Q15(a) • Unit VExplain construction, salient functional features and working of an interferometer.

Exam sketch: draw coherent source, beam splitter, reference arm, measurement arm, reflector, recombination and detector, then mark the optical path difference.

Construction and working: An interferometer contains a coherent light source, beam splitter, reference path, measurement path, reflector/mirror, recombination optics and detector. The two optical paths recombine and produce interference. Change in path length causes a phase/fringe change, allowing precise displacement measurement.

Functional features: high resolution, non-contact measurement, traceable wavelength reference and suitability for displacement/alignment/calibration. Environmental compensation may be required for accurate long-range work.

Nov/Dec 2024 • Q16(a) • Unit IIDifferentiate major, minor and effective diameter of a screw thread and explain common measurement techniques.

Major diameter: diameter measured over the thread crests of an external thread (or corresponding crest limit for an internal thread). Minor diameter: diameter at the thread roots. Effective/pitch diameter: diameter of an imaginary cylinder where thread thickness and space are equal under the specified pitch/geometry condition.

Measurement techniques: screw-thread micrometer, three-wire method, two-wire method, pitch-diameter gauges and optical/profile methods. The three-wire method places three precision wires in the thread grooves and measures the over-wire dimension; pitch diameter is calculated from the measured value, wire diameter and thread geometry.

Nov/Dec 2024 • Q16(b) • Unit IIDevelop the method for inspection of straightness of a machine-tool guideway using an autocollimator and reference line through first/last points.
  1. Clean and establish the guideway reference points.
  2. Set the autocollimator at a suitable position and establish the zero/reference condition.
  3. Place the reflecting target/mirror at successive equally spaced stations along the guideway.
  4. Record the angular deviation at each station.
  5. Convert angular readings to linear deviation using the appropriate instrument scale factor/geometry.
  6. Choose the reference line passing through the first and last points, as requested.
  7. Subtract the straight reference line from the measured profile values to obtain straightness error at each station.
  8. Plot station position versus corrected deviation. The maximum separation between the reference and measured profile gives the straightness error under the adopted evaluation convention.
The key exam point is: measure angular deviations → convert to displacement → construct first-to-last reference line → calculate deviations from that line → obtain maximum straightness error.
