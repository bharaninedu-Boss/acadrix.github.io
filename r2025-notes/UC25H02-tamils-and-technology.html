Course approach

Theory · L-T-P-C: 1-0-0-1

This course studies selected technologies and knowledge systems associated with Tamil society, including the Sangam age and later periods. The official CSE curriculum organises it into five syllabus areas. Use the five headings below as your revision framework.

Answer-writing method: For each technology, explain (1) what it is, (2) its material or working principle, (3) the historical/Tamil context named in the syllabus, and (4) the evidence or present-day relevance. Use a labelled sketch or comparison table when it genuinely helps.

Important distinction: Archaeological evidence, literary references and later architectural examples are different kinds of sources. Do not present an interpretation as a proven fact unless your prescribed text or teacher supports it.

1. Weaving and Ceramic Technology

Weaving industry during the Sangam Age

Weaving converts fibres into cloth by interlacing yarns. The two basic yarn directions are warp (lengthwise threads held under tension) and weft (threads passed across the warp). A loom maintains the warp and helps interlace the weft. When studying the Sangam-age weaving industry, organise notes around raw materials, yarn preparation, loom/weaving process, finished textiles, craft specialisation and trade references found in prescribed sources.

TermMeaningWhat to show in an answer
FibreBasic textile material from which yarn is madeSource/material named in your reference

Ceramic technology

Ceramics are objects made from clay or other inorganic materials, shaped and hardened by firing. A useful process outline is clay selection and preparation → shaping → drying → surface treatment → firing → finished pottery. The syllabus specifically names Black and Red Ware (BRW) and graffiti on potteries.

  • Black and Red Ware: a pottery category identified by its characteristic black and red surface appearance. In an exam answer, describe the ware and the firing/surface features as explained in the prescribed archaeological material; avoid assuming every BRW object has identical manufacture.
  • Graffiti on pottery: marks or symbols made on pottery surfaces. Record the difference between a mark/symbol and a fully deciphered written inscription. Explain how pottery finds and markings can help archaeologists study material culture and exchange.
Diagram practice: Draw a simple loom showing warp and weft. Draw a pottery-making sequence with shaping, drying and firing. Add a labelled sketch or photograph of BRW only from an authorised class/textbook source.

2. Design and Construction Technology

House design and structural construction in the Sangam Age

Study the curriculum's focus on house designs, structural construction and household materials. Structure your answer around site/use, available building materials, components, construction method and how the building suited its environment. Use examples specifically described in your course material; do not generalise one excavated or literary example to every settlement.

Building materials and hero stones

Building materials are selected for their availability, strength, durability and suitability for the intended structure. The syllabus also names hero stones (நடுகல்). A hero stone is a commemorative stone associated with the remembrance of a person, commonly in the context explained by Tamil historical sources. Describe its form, imagery, inscription (if present) and memorial significance using the prescribed examples.

Stage construction in Silappathikaram

The course asks students to study details of stage construction described in Silappathikaram. Prepare the terminology, arrangement and construction features actually covered in your prescribed text. Relate the stage to performance requirements and audience visibility where supported by the text.

3. Manufacturing Technology

Shipbuilding

The syllabus includes the art of shipbuilding. Study the construction materials, hull/structural arrangement, joining methods and the relationship between vessel design and its intended use, based on the historical sources assigned by your teacher. Connect shipbuilding with maritime trade only where the reference provides evidence.

Metallurgy, iron and steel

Metallurgy is the science and technology of extracting metals from ores and processing them into useful forms. The prescribed topic includes metallurgical studies, the iron industry, iron smelting and steel. A general process map for understanding metal production is ore → preparation → extraction/smelting → refining or processing → shaping into an artefact. Your answer should distinguish the evidence for a process from a modern explanation of the process.

Coins as historical sources

The curriculum names copper and gold coins and coin minting. Coins can provide evidence about rulers, symbols, trade, metal use, circulation and political authority. Explain minting as the production of coins and identify the specific coin examples in your class material.

Bead-making industries

Study the listed materials separately: stone beads, glass beads, terracotta beads, shell beads and bone beads. Compare raw material, shaping/working, finishing and what archaeological finds can reveal about craft production and exchange.

Archaeological evidence and gemstones in Silappathikaram

Prepare how artefacts, production remains and excavated contexts help reconstruct manufacturing activity. The syllabus also asks for gemstone types described in Silappathikaram; learn the names and descriptions from the prescribed text rather than substituting a generic gemstone list.

4. Agriculture and Irrigation Technology

Water-storage and distribution structures

The syllabus explicitly names dams, tanks, ponds and sluices. Explain their functions and how they fit into water storage and controlled distribution.

  • Dam: a barrier constructed across a watercourse to store or regulate water.
  • Tank: a managed water-storage structure, commonly used to collect and retain water for local needs including irrigation.
  • Pond: a smaller water body or storage feature; explain its specific use from the prescribed example.
  • Sluice: a controlled outlet or passage used to regulate the release of stored water.

Kumizhi Thoompu of the Chola period

This is a named syllabus topic. Learn its structure, location in the water-management system, operating purpose and historical example from the prescribed reference. Explain why controlled water release matters for irrigation. Do not draw an assumed mechanism if the prescribed diagram or explanation differs.

Animal husbandry and wells for cattle

Study animal husbandry as the care and management of domesticated animals and the syllabus topic of wells designed for cattle use. Explain how water access, livestock care and agricultural life are connected in the examples taught.

Agriculture and agro-processing

Prepare the agricultural activities and post-harvest processing methods described in your course material. A useful framework is cultivation → harvesting → cleaning/sorting → processing → storage → distribution. Add only the crops, tools and processing examples explicitly discussed in class.

Sea knowledge, fisheries, pearls and conch diving

The course connects maritime knowledge with fisheries, pearl and conch diving, and ancient knowledge of the ocean. Study coastal occupations, the skills and equipment described in historical sources, and the economic/social significance of marine resources. Distinguish literary references from archaeological or other material evidence.

5. Scientific Tamil and Tamil Computing

Development of Scientific Tamil

Scientific Tamil concerns expressing scientific and technical concepts accurately in Tamil. Study the development of technical vocabulary, translation/adaptation of concepts and the need for consistent terminology in education and public communication.

Tamil computing

Tamil computing applies digital technologies to Tamil text and language. The syllabus lists:

  • Digitalisation of Tamil books.
  • Development of Tamil software.
  • Tamil Virtual Academy.
  • Tamil Digital Library.
  • Online Tamil dictionaries.
  • Sorkuvai Project (சொற்குவை), named in the curriculum.

Prepare short notes on the purpose and usefulness of each resource. Examples include preserving texts in digital form, enabling search and access, supporting Tamil typing and processing, and making vocabulary resources available online. For specific features of each named institution/project, use its official description rather than guessing.

A good comparison answer can distinguish digitisation (converting existing material into digital form) from digital language tools (software and services that enable searching, typing, processing or learning).

6. Revision checklist and practice questions

Checklist

  • Weaving: warp, weft, loom, Sangam-age weaving industry.
  • Ceramics: pottery-making sequence, Black and Red Ware, graffiti on pottery.
  • Construction: house design, building materials, hero stones, Silappathikaram stage construction and all named architectural case studies.
  • Manufacturing: shipbuilding, metallurgy, iron/steel, coins and minting, bead industries, archaeological evidence and gemstones in Silappathikaram.
  • Agriculture: dams, tanks, ponds, sluices, Kumizhi Thoompu, animal husbandry, cattle wells, agro-processing, fisheries, pearl/conch diving and ocean knowledge.
  • Scientific Tamil: scientific terminology, Tamil computing, digitisation, Tamil software, Tamil Virtual Academy, Tamil Digital Library, online dictionaries and Sorkuvai.

Syllabus-aligned practice questions

  1. Describe the weaving industry during the Sangam Age and explain the basic working principle of a loom.
  2. Explain ceramic technology and discuss Black and Red Ware and graffiti on potteries.
  3. Discuss house design and structural construction during the Sangam Age with reference to materials.
  4. Write notes on hero stones and the stage construction details described in Silappathikaram.
  5. Explain the architectural features of the Madurai Meenakshi Temple and Thirumalai Nayakar Mahal as covered in the prescribed case study.
  6. Discuss the development of iron smelting and steel production with reference to the historical evidence in your course material.
  7. Explain the importance of coins as historical sources and describe minting.
  8. Compare stone, glass, terracotta, shell and bone bead-making industries.
  9. Explain the role of dams, tanks, ponds and sluices in irrigation.
  10. Describe the significance of Kumizhi Thoompu in Chola-period water management.
  11. Discuss the place of fisheries, pearl diving and conch diving in maritime knowledge.
  12. Explain the development of Scientific Tamil and the importance of Tamil computing resources named in the syllabus.
Exam note: These are practice questions created from the official topic list, not predicted or guaranteed university questions. Your faculty's notes, prescribed books and question-paper format determine the expected answer depth.
