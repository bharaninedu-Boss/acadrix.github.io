1. PH25C03 — Applied Physics (CSIE) – II

Theory · 2-1-0 · 3 credits (as listed in the CSE Semester II table)

How to study this paper: It combines magnetic materials, digital logic, nano-devices and quantum computing. Prepare the physical principle first, then the device/application, then a labelled diagram or comparison table.

Unit area: Magnetic materials and data storage

  • Magnetic materials: classify materials by their magnetic response; distinguish diamagnetic, paramagnetic and ferromagnetic behaviour using susceptibility and domain ideas.
  • Ferromagnetic materials and ferrites: understand domain alignment, hysteresis behaviour and why ferrites are useful in high-frequency applications.
  • GMR sensors: explain giant magnetoresistance as a resistance change associated with magnetic-layer alignment; connect the effect with magnetic sensing and data-reading applications.
  • Magnetic disk memories: study recording, storage and read/write concepts at the level prescribed by the course.

Unit area: Digital electronics foundations

  • Revise binary and decimal number systems and conversions.
  • Understand basic logic gates and their truth tables.
  • Represent Boolean functions in SOP and POS forms and simplify them.
  • Use Karnaugh maps, including don't-care conditions, to obtain simplified logic expressions.
  • Practise drawing the logic circuit corresponding to a simplified Boolean expression.
Worked method — K-map: (1) Write the function in minterm/maxterm form. (2) Draw the correctly ordered map. (3) Enter 1s for SOP or 0s for POS. (4) Include don't-care cells only when they help form larger valid groups. (5) Make groups in powers of two and wrap around edges where allowed. (6) Write the reduced expression and verify it against the original truth table.

Unit area: Nano-devices and quantum confinement

  • Relate electron density and Fermi energy to the electronic behaviour of a material.
  • Explain quantum confinement and distinguish quantum wells, quantum wires and quantum dots by the dimensions in which carriers are confined.
  • Understand tunnelling and Coulomb blockade at a conceptual level.
  • Study the single-electron transistor (SET), resonant tunnelling diode and carbon nanotubes as nanoscale devices/materials.

Unit area: Quantum computing

  • Define a qubit and compare its state description with a classical bit.
  • Interpret the Bloch sphere as a geometric representation of a pure single-qubit state.
  • Learn the action and matrix form of Pauli-X, Pauli-Y, Pauli-Z, Hadamard, Phase and T gates from class notes.
  • Explain the CNOT gate and its role in two-qubit operations and entanglement.
  • Discuss why quantum computing can offer advantages for selected problem classes; do not claim that it is faster for every task.

Practice questions

  1. Explain the magnetic behaviour of ferromagnetic materials and the role of ferrites.
  2. Describe the principle and applications of a GMR sensor.
  3. Explain magnetic disk recording and storage.
  4. Simplify a Boolean function using a K-map with don't-care terms and draw its logic circuit.
  5. Explain quantum confinement and compare wells, wires and dots.
  6. Describe the operating idea of a single-electron transistor.
  7. Explain a qubit using the Bloch sphere and describe the action of the Hadamard and CNOT gates.
Scope note: The official course syllabus names the topics above. Detailed derivations, numerical limits, device construction diagrams and exact unit boundaries should be taken from the current faculty notes/textbook; they are not specified in this short learning guide.

2. EN25C02 — English Essentials – II

Integrated · 1-0-2 · 2 credits

This is a communication-and-professional-writing course. Prepare by practising the output formats, not by memorising definitions alone.

Communication and presentation

  • Identify interpersonal and intrapersonal communication and explain common communication barriers.
  • Practise summarising information presented through visuals and media.
  • Recognise media terminology and rhetorical devices used to persuade or emphasise.
  • Prepare a short presentation or speech and learn how to discuss a TED Talk or podcast critically.

Correspondence and career documents

  • Use modal verbs appropriately to express ability, obligation, permission and possibility.
  • Prepare a professional job-application letter and a concise, role-focused resume.
  • Draft a statement of purpose (SOP) with a clear motivation, relevant preparation and future goal.
  • Paraphrase and summarise source material accurately without changing its meaning.
  • Write an executive summary that presents the purpose, key findings and recommendations of a longer document.
  • Practise formal email structure, subject lines, salutations, clear requests and courteous closing.

Professional writing

  • Proposal: state the problem or opportunity, objective, proposed action, resources/time and expected outcome.
  • Decision-making: define the decision, identify criteria, compare alternatives and justify the selected option.
  • Recommendation: connect evidence to a practical action and state why it is suitable.
  • Report: use a clear title, purpose, method/background, findings, discussion and conclusion/recommendations as required by the task.

Teamwork and workplace communication

  • Understand leadership styles, negotiation and constructive disagreement.
  • Prepare a SWOT analysis: strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats.
  • Practise mock meetings, group discussions and brainstorming; contribute clearly and listen actively.

Practice tasks

  1. Write a formal email requesting permission to conduct a technical workshop.
  2. Prepare a one-page resume for an entry-level engineering internship using only accurate details.
  3. Write an SOP for applying to a technical training programme.
  4. Prepare a short proposal for improving a laboratory facility.
  5. Summarise a short talk and identify two rhetorical devices used by the speaker.
  6. Prepare a SWOT analysis for a student project team.
  7. Conduct a mock meeting with agenda, minutes and action items.
Scoring tip: Follow the requested format exactly. Marks can be lost through missing subject lines, unclear paragraphing, unsupported claims, spelling errors or failure to address the task.

3. ME25C05 — Re-Engineering for Innovation

Laboratory / practical · 0-0-4 · 2 credits

The curriculum describes a hands-on cycle connecting product development, reverse engineering, CAD, embedded systems, prototyping and assembly. Maintain a project record with evidence at each stage.

Practical workflow

  1. Choose and understand a product: identify its purpose, users, main functions and constraints. Record the original product's limitations.
  2. Disassemble safely: photograph the product before and during disassembly, label parts and record the order of removal. Do not force-fit or damage components.
  3. Measure and sketch: identify datum surfaces, measure accessible dimensions with suitable instruments, and create component sketches with units and tolerances where specified.
  4. Create CAD models: model individual components, name files systematically and check dimensions against the measured sketches.
  5. Perform virtual assembly: apply appropriate mates/constraints, check alignment and interference, and document assembly issues.
  6. Identify an improvement: use brainstorming to propose alternatives. Compare concepts against function, safety, cost, manufacturability and sustainability.
  7. Embedded-system activity: where required, select sensors, identify input/output signals, develop the programme and record the observed data. The curriculum gives home, healthcare and agriculture automation as application contexts.
  8. Prototype: prepare a sketch or CAD design, select a feasible manufacturing route, and fabricate using the permitted 3D-printing or workshop process.
  9. Assemble and test: check fit, function and safety. Record failures, modifications and final test observations.
  10. Present: explain the original product, identified problem, redesign, prototype, test result and learning.

Suggested practical record format

SectionWhat to record
AimSpecific reverse-engineering or redesign objective
Product detailsName, function, user, existing design and constraints
Tools/materialsMeasuring tools, CAD software, components and fabrication tools actually used
ProcedureDisassembly, measurement, modelling, redesign and assembly steps
EvidencePhotographs, dimensioned sketches, CAD screenshots and prototype images
ResultsDimensions, fit checks, sensor readings or test observations
ConclusionWhether the objective was achieved and what should be improved next

Viva preparation

  1. What is reverse engineering, and how is it different from copying a product?
  2. Why should parts be labelled during disassembly?
  3. What is the purpose of a datum in measurement and modelling?
  4. Why are CAD constraints or mates used in virtual assembly?
  5. What is an interference check?
  6. How do you select a concept for prototyping?
  7. What is the difference between a sensor and an actuator?
  8. What evidence should be collected during prototype testing?
Practical safety: Disconnect electrical power before opening equipment. Use workshop tools, soldering equipment and 3D printers only under the laboratory's safety instructions and supervision. Do not bypass guards or test mains-powered circuits without authorisation.
